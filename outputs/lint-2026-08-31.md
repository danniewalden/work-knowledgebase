## Finding 1 — Yesterday's ingest contradicted itself in three places ⚠ the real issue

Three pages written *within hours of each other today* assert things another page written the same day
explicitly denies. All three are mine, all three are now fixed, and they share one cause.

**1a. "Both camps reject Markdown."** `model-as-code-vs-model-as-language` carries a framing caution
saying exactly this must not be claimed — Miller never discusses prose or Markdown, and in fact lists "a
markdown input?" among options he'd consider. `spec-driven-development` then said: *"Markdown-as-
specification… is the thing that fails… Three independent critiques, one target."* The page made the
inference the other page was written to forbid.

**1b. Shipped vs proposed.** `model-as-code-vs-model-as-language`: *"This is a proposal, not shipped
product."* `agent-readable-model-artifacts`, same day: Miller *"ships the alternative"* and has *"shipping
tooling."*

**1c. The rubric.** `token-budget-quality-cliff`: *"he does not say the rubric is what surfaced it."*
`event-modeled-agent-design`, same day: *"he only saw it because he had a rubric"* — presented as that
section's headline argument.

**The cause is worth recording, because it will recur.** In each case a *careful* qualification was
written on the page nearest the source, and then a *tidier* version of the same claim was written on the
page that needed the claim to do argumentative work. Compiling one source into six pages means the caveat
has to be re-earned six times, and it degrades a little each hop. The fix that generalizes: when a source
page hedges something, the hedge belongs in the sentence the concept pages will quote, not in a caveats
section they'll skip.

**All three corrected**, with the corrections written as explicit "be careful with this inference" notes
rather than silent edits, so the next pass sees why.

## Finding 2 — The Ng primacy claim was wrong in 8 places (corrected)

Yesterday's hunt captured three SDD critiques that all predate Alvis Ng's essay by four to five months —
Böckeler 2025-10-15, Zaninotto 2025-11-12, Eberhardt 2025-11-26 — and each cites the one before it. Ng is
the downstream synthesis of an **Oct–Nov 2025 critique wave**, not its origin. The wiki said otherwise in
eight places, all now corrected: `index.md` ×3, `spec-driven-development` ×4, `entities/alvis-ng`,
`entities/birgitta-bockeler`, `overview.md` ×2.

Three factual errors fell out of the same comparison, all now checkable against the primaries and all
fixed:

- *"Scott Logic ran SpecKit on a real production codebase"* — it was Colin Eberhardt (Scott Logic's CTO)
  on his own **hobby app**, a go-kart lap-time tracker.
- *"the spec phase alone produced 2,500 lines of Markdown"* — wrong slice of the number. 2,577 is the
  **cumulative** total for the whole feature; the specify step was 230 lines. The bloated step was
  *plan*, at 2,067.
- *"the Fowler/Böckeler Kiro analysis"* — **Böckeler is the sole author**; Fowler's site is the venue.
  This one propagated from Ng's own mislabelling.

**What is deliberately not fixed:** `spec-driven-development`'s section *framing* still treats Ng as the
assembler of the empirical record. Rewriting that properly means writing the page from the three
primaries, which is an ingest, not a lint. The section now opens with a boxed chronology correction
saying so.

Also recovered: Böckeler's article is **the origin of the spec-first / spec-anchored / spec-as-source
taxonomy** the KB uses as working vocabulary without attributing it, and it carries an **MDD parallel**
nothing else in the KB holds — spec-as-source may inherit "the downsides of both MDD and LLMs:
inflexibility and non-determinism," and MDD's *parseable* structure at least bought tool support for
writing valid, complete, consistent specs. That is simultaneously the best historical prior for the
model-as-language position and the sharpest warning against it, and it belongs on
`model-as-code-vs-model-as-language` the moment that page's Batch-G ingest runs.

## Finding 3 — Two concepts are now overdue their own page

Not "mentioned once somewhere" — these are load-bearing vocabulary.

**`slice` — the biggest gap in the wiki.** 37 concept/entity pages use the word; ~20 wikilink it, and
every one of those is aliased to `[[vertical-slice-architecture]]`. That alias hides a conflation: Bogard's
VSA slice is a *code-organisation* pattern, while Event Modeling's slice is a unit of the *model* — a
command/view/automation plus its GWT. Every agent-facing claim in the KB rests on the second: "unit of
work is the EM slice," "slice = unit of work, GWT = the verification rubric," "one slice = one agent's
context," pay-per-slice pricing, build-time-per-slice as an estimation unit. The spine for the page is
sitting un-ingested — `raw/notes/dilger-element-slice-chapter-story-context-ladder` gives the explicit
**Element → Slice → Chapter → Story → Context** ladder. Write it with Batch D; fold in `chapter` and
`storyline`.

**`triplet-architecture`** — 11 pages, 45 occurrences, a dedicated source page, and Dilger now hashtags
it. Flagged by yesterday's lint too. It has earned a page and needs no new source.

**`ubiquitous-language`** — now a live broken link from two pages (I added the second today). Marginal
from wiki material alone; the strong grounding is un-ingested (`macmanus-pocock-wayfinder-skill-fog-of-war`,
where Pocock arrives at DDD from the agent-skills side: *"I realized that I needed a ubiquitous language
between me and the agent"*). Write it with Batch F 2b, not before.

**`harness absorption` — zero wiki presence, and the page that should hold it can't.** McAteer's thesis
(models absorb the harness into their weights; engineers delete what got absorbed; what remains is a
harness for *human attention*) appears nowhere in `wiki/`. `agent-harness.md` is 61 lines, the smallest
page in its thread, untouched since 2026-07-27, and holds none of it — nor the Harness-Bench figure
(same model, same 106 tasks, 52.4 → 76.2), which is the only hard quantification of "half the agent is
the harness" anywhere in the corpus. This is the strongest argument for running Batch F.

**Do not write `scenario-hunting`.** One LinkedIn comment is the entire evidence base. Chase it on a live
sweep instead.

## Finding 4 — Caveat discipline is inconsistent, and it favours the sources the KB likes

The KB caveats Dilger, AxonIQ and Akka carefully. It does not apply the same standard to:

- **Stripe minions** — "1,000+ merged per week with no human-written code" is repeated bare on
  `unattended-coding-agents`, and its source page calls it "the strongest production datapoint in the
  cluster" with **no vendor caveat at all**. It is Stripe's engineering blog about Stripe's own tooling.
- **Bayer PRINCE** — the source page carries the caveat ("Thoughtworks-authored, the firm that coined
  'harness engineering'"), but `agent-explainability` calls it **"independent corroboration"** and
  `harness-engineering` lists it with no caveat. It is not independent of the concept's author's employer.
- **Thoughtworks Radar** — cited flat as evidence on `model-context-protocol`, while
  `business-capabilities` says on the same day that Radar citations by a Thoughtworks author "are not
  independent corroboration."
- **AHE** — called "the peer-review-grade primary" on two pages; its own source page says "preprint."
  Same for ESAA ("peer-style" → "academic source"). Neither is peer-reviewed.
- **Eberhardt's ~10x** — hardened into "the first captured datum" and "the first such number." It is a
  self-reported impression followed by the author's own caveats section. *(Corrected today.)*

`entities/thoughtworks.md` has been rewritten to name this concentration explicitly — it was 8 lines,
81 days stale, listed 1 of 7 sources, and described the firm as one person's employer while four tracked
people work there.
