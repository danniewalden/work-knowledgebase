## Finding 1 — 46 captures cite a mutable feed instead of the post. **Open, mostly unfixable retroactively.**

The 2026-09-04 research run flagged this as "five Tornhill and two Khononov entries." It is
**46 captures**, and the shape is worse than a filename problem: their `source_url` points at
a `recent-activity/all/` feed or a Substack `/archive`, which is a pointer at *whatever that
feed shows today*. The claim becomes unverifiable as soon as the author posts again.

Distribution: **Dilger 28**, Fritzsche 7, Dymitruk 3, Tornhill 3 (one of them the Substack
archive), plus Goeleven, Tune, Khononov, Fraktalio, Adaptech and Johansen. Not confined to
`raw/notes/` either — six are in `raw/articles/`, where the filename implies a fetched article.

Three of the 46 are the honest form and should be the model for the rest: they state the
limitation *inside* the `source_url` (`"…/recent-activity/all/ (permalink not captured; ~1 day
old at retrieval)"`). The other 43 state a feed URL as though it were the source.

- **Not being rewritten retroactively** — each post would have to be re-found individually,
  and for older ones the 5-post feed cap makes that impossible without a search.
- **Fixed going forward.** All 20 captures filed by today's sweep use canonical
  `linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:<id>/` permalinks, and `CLAUDE.md` now carries the
  convention, the extraction rule that produces it, and the honest-fallback form.
- **Consequence to remember:** a feed-URL capture has a weaker citation chain than its
  filename suggests. Re-resolve one before leaning on it hard.

## Finding 3 — 45 fidelity defects in the new pages. **All fixed.**

An adversarial pass re-read all 90 raw captures against the pages compiled from them. 41 pages
were clean; 49 had at least one defect. The classes, worst first:

1. **Four fabricated quotes** — text in quotation marks that appears nowhere in the cited raw.
   One attributed a quote to the wrong Fritzsche article (it was in the sequel); one invented an
   AxonIQ marketing line; one quote-marked a paraphrase drawn from a different capture entirely;
   one spliced two separate Tornhill passages into a single quotation.
2. **Two invented numbers** — "twenty-plus years in this corner of Python" from a source that
   says only "a long time", and an "early in his career / decades old" dating on an anecdote the
   raw never dates.
3. **A hedge deleted inside a quotation** — "didn't seem to hold" for the raw's "didn't seem to
   hold *as strongly as it did back in 2025*", turning a comparative into an absolute.
4. **"The ratio is the datum"** — the exact promotion CLAUDE.md forbids, applied to a
   practitioner's self-report about his own unreviewed code, on a page that marked it
   IMPRESSION NOT MEASUREMENT two lines later.
5. **Survey ordering reversed** — two pages claimed Guo et al. was the first of two independent
   surveys "two months apart". Ning et al. is the earlier (2026-05-18 vs 2026-06-14) and the gap
   is 27 days. Propagated to `index.md` and `harness-engineering`; all corrected.
6. **The un-verifiable phrase reproduced by the page banning it** — `agent-engineering` printed
   Cao's three-role phrasing verbatim in quotation marks inside the sentence forbidding the
   quote. Exactly the copy-paste vector the rule exists to close.
7. **Twelve stale self-referential claims** — source pages asserting the present state of a
   concept page ("the loop-engineering page currently asserts X as settled") which the same
   session then changed. Reworded to past tense so the substance survives without the false claim.
8. Assorted: two wrong illustration counts, three word-count overstatements (one 40% high), a
   source's own typo silently corrected inside a quotation, a future conference described in the
   past tense, and a tally of "three of five definitional sources" that the five sources do not
   support (it is two of three, by name).

Notably, no page misreported a source's *figure* — every number traced correctly. The failures
were of quotation discipline, dating, arithmetic and confidence, not of data.
