**2. "Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses" — Lin/Liu/Dou/Gui et al. (Fudan/Peking), arXiv 2604.25850** · *flagged 07-13, NOT yet captured (out-of-window, Apr 2026)*
- **TLDR:** A closed-loop system where an agent **autonomously evolves its own harness** via three observability pillars (component / experience / decision), with every edit framed as a falsifiable prediction. 10 iterations lift Terminal-Bench-2 pass@1 from 69.7→77.0% (beating human-designed Codex-CLI at 71.9% plus ACE/TF-GRPO baselines) and transfer to SWE-bench-verified at 75.6% with −12% tokens.
- **Why it's worth reading:** It's the empirical, peer-review-grade realization of the hill-climbing / self-improving loop (LangChain's 4th stacked loop) — the primary your loop-engineering thread currently lacks, sitting on top of practitioner essays. This is the one to *capture* next.

**3. Dilger — "How I use Local-LLMs to build software systems" (2-part)** · `raw/notes/dilger-local-llm-distributed-agent-setup-event-modeling.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
- **TLDR:** The most concrete implementation report yet of Dilger's always-on Event Modeling agent factory: 6–10 agents in 24/7 ralph-loops across 3 Asus GX10 boxes (no clustering), harnessed by Claude Code / OpenCode over Ollama (Gemma4 ~60 tok/s, Qwen3.6:27B ~30 tok/s). Model a slice → set "Planned" → one agent claims it and flips it "In Progress" (single-writer lock; others skip) → builds it; move a slice "Done"→"Planned" and an agent diffs spec-vs-code and adjusts. "At some point you stop thinking about the code." Calls EM + Event Sourcing + slice-based architecture "the triplet of flexible architectures."
- **Why it's worth reading:** Your Event-Modeling-Agent-Harness idea running on real, cheap hardware — the operational "how" (models, throughput, claim/lock mechanism) the earlier conceptual posts left abstract.

**4. Dilger — "Extending Event Modeling: an optional 'Query' (WHEN) on the read side"** · `raw/notes/dilger-extending-event-modeling-query-when.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
- **TLDR:** Notable because Dilger — scrupulous about sticking to Dymitruk's EM "Standard" while writing his book and building the Eventmodelers platform — **extends the notation for the first time**. After the topic came up independently in several Munich conversations (and pre-discussed with Dymitruk), he adds an **optional WHEN ("Query") clause on the read side** so read-model GWT scenarios can express complex queries (`GIVEN user registered / WHEN we query by email / THEN the user is returned`), not just GIVEN…THEN. He frames it cautiously, is explicitly seeking community feedback, and notes early positive reception.
- **Why it's worth reading:** A rare method-level change to Event Modeling from a core practitioner, straight out of the 2026 conference — worth knowing before it propagates into the tooling and GWT-generation you rely on.

**5. Osmani — "Own the Outer Loop"** · `raw/articles/addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop.md` · *filed 07-12, not yet ingested*
- **TLDR:** Osmani's follow-on to his loop-engineering primary: as agents take the *inner* execution loop, engineers must own the *outer* loop — accountability — via **Quality** (back-pressure checks that emit evidence), **Verdict** (the human ship/block/redirect call), and **Answerability** (guaranteeing you can explain *why*). Humans belong in the constraints/sampling/audit/ownership loops, not the inner one; a "factory" is loops at scale. Backed by fresh data: Sonar 2026 (42% of committed code AI-assisted), GitLab Jun-2026 (governance happens *after* creation), an Anthropic RCT (AI-assisted engineers score 17pp lower on comprehension), Wharton (people accept wrong AI ~75% of the time, with *more* confidence). "The agent can ship more than you can review."
- **Why it's worth reading:** It sharpens "stay the engineer" into a concrete accountability contract — the human-boundary counterpart to Dilger's autonomous factory (#3), and it pairs directly with the just-ingested Willison DRI note.

**6. Khononov — "Remember the microservices hype 12 years ago? (the AI '(s)loop' as the new Holy Grail)"** · `raw/notes/khononov-microservices-hype-to-ai-sloop.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
