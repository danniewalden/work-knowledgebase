## Read next

**1. Osmani — "Software Factories, Light and Dark"** (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-20) · `raw/articles/addyosmani-software-factories-light-and-dark.md` · *filed 07-23, not yet ingested*
**TLDR:** Osmani stacks three concepts — **loop → harness → factory** ("the loop is the atom; the factory is the loop at scale… an org chart made of loops"). He splits factories into **light vs dark**: a *dark* factory ships code no human has read, verified only by machines (borrowed from FANUC/Xiaomi lights-out manufacturing — "in software, the floor is the diff"); a *lit* factory keeps humans in where judgment lives and moves review **upstream** to product/design/architecture. Core argument: **verification, not generation, is the constraint** — "back pressure" = hand a loop only as much autonomy as you can cheaply and reliably verify. A loop "earns the dark" only with a cheap, high-frequency, un-fakeable green/red oracle and 3–10 short steps (Dex Horthy's rule). Closes on **loops vs graphs vs state machines** — "owning your control flow is really just walking the graph back around the loop" (cites LangGraph, LlamaIndex Workflows, Jerry Liu, David Khourshid) — directly answering the "graph engineering" framing flagged on 07-20 and tying to [[nick-tune-graphs-memory-skills-agents]]. Leans heavily on **Dex Horthy / HumanLayer**'s AIEWF talk *"Harness Engineering is not Enough."*
**Why it's worth reading:** This is the top item — the newest keeper and the first primary to name the **factory** rung above [[loop-engineering]], plus the sharpest statement yet that human judgment moves *upstream* (the outer-loop / [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]] thesis extended). Directly on your loop-eng focus and it resolves the graphs-vs-loops question the watch has been circling.

**2. Osmani — "Earning taste and judgment"** (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-14) · `raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment.md` · *filed 07-21, not yet ingested*
**TLDR:** The companion piece: agents automate the "reps" that used to produce taste, so **taste/judgment is now the durable, ungradeable human contribution** and juniors must acquire it deliberately. Restates the **inner-loop (agent: investigate/implement/test/report) vs outer-loop (human: worth-attention? verify by evidence → approve/block → carry consequence)** boundary; names **two debts — cognitive surrender + comprehension** (ties [[willison-understand-to-participate]]). Gives **7 concrete habits**, of which two land on your substrate: *"build an eval/rubric… run on 50 real AI PRs"* (the grader/verification loop as personal calibration) and *"specify and verify separately — specification quality is the biggest lever"* (the spec-driven seam). Marshals labor-market data (NY Fed, Stanford Digital Economy Lab, WEF) and cites Kent Beck, Boris Cherny, Anthropic's paradox-of-supervision.
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the un-ingested companion to #1 from the lead loop-eng voice, and its "specify + verify separately, spec quality is the biggest lever" line is a direct bridge from loop-engineering to your Event-Modeling-as-spec focus.

**3. arXiv 2604.25850 — "Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses"** (Fudan/Peking, Apr 2026) · *referenced repeatedly in the watch log; NOT yet captured to `raw/`*
**TLDR (from the watch notes):** A closed-loop system where an agent **autonomously evolves its own harness** via three observability pillars (component / experience / decision), each edit a falsifiable prediction; 10 iterations lift Terminal-Bench-2 pass@1 69.7→77.0% (beating human-designed Codex-CLI 71.9% + ACE/TF-GRPO baselines) and transfer to SWE-bench-verified 75.6% at −12% tokens.
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the **peer-review-grade empirical realization of LangChain's 4th "hill-climbing" loop** — the one primary the otherwise vendor/practitioner-heavy loop-engineering thread lacks. It would give [[loop-engineering]] its first hard evidence base. (Read = capture first; it's out-of-window so the watch won't pull it automatically.)

**4. Dex Horthy / HumanLayer — "Harness Engineering is not Enough: Why Software Factories Fail"** (AI Engineer World's Fair talk, youtu.be/htM02KMNZnk) · *referenced as the backbone of item #1; NOT captured; flagged as a new candidate entity*
**TLDR:** The talk Osmani's software-factory piece is built on: running a **fully automated dark factory for ~4 months with no human reading the code** produced a major, hard-to-debug failure; the fix is the light/dark switch, back-pressure, and the wiring diagram (queue → harness → automated checks → review gate → deploy → monitoring → signals) where the review gate is the one expensive, non-scaling box. Source of the "3–10 step loop," "most agents are mostly deterministic code with LLM steps sprinkled in," and the nightly one-anti-pattern-per-PR cron example.
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the upstream primary for the whole software-factory framing and introduces **Dex Horthy / HumanLayer (12-factor-agents)** as a voice worth tracking. A talk transcript may be hard to capture headless, but it's the source-of-record for the thread.
