**1. Software Factories, Light and Dark — Addy Osmani (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-20)** · `raw/articles/addyosmani-software-factories-light-and-dark` · *filed, not yet ingested*
TLDR: Osmani stacks the vocabulary into three layers — **loop** (one agent on repeat: gather→act→check→repeat), **harness** (the walls: sandbox, tools, memory, done-gates), and **factory** (many harnessed loops fed by a queue and drained through one review gate, "an org chart made of loops, not a bigger agent"). A **dark factory** ships code no human has read, verified only by machines (borrowing FANUC/Xiaomi lights-out manufacturing; "in software, the floor is the diff") — easy at first, but it silently takes on **comprehension debt** "with the tests green the whole way." The fix is a **lit factory**: move human judgment *upstream* to product/design/architecture, and keep the lights on wherever a wrong call is expensive. Core rule is **back pressure** — hand a loop only as much autonomy as you can *cheaply and reliably verify*; verification (the narrow neck), not generation (the wide mouth), is the real constraint. A loop "earns the dark" only with a cheap, high-frequency, un-fakeable green/red oracle and short 3–10 step runs (Dex Horthy's rule). Closes on **loops vs graphs**: "owning your control flow is really just walking the graph back around the loop" — the flowchart we tried to throw away (LangGraph, LlamaIndex Workflows, Khourshid's state-machines-in-new-clothes).
Why it's worth reading: this is the piece that extends `[[loop-engineering]]` up one more rung to the *factory*, and it directly answers the "graph engineering" framing you flagged on 07-20 — the sharpest current statement of the environment-over-model / back-pressure through-line running through your harness and loop threads.

**2. Earning taste and judgment — Addy Osmani (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-14)** · `raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment` · *filed, not yet ingested*
TLDR: The companion post to #1. Taste and judgment used to be a byproduct of "the reps" (boilerplate, bug fixes); agents now take the reps, so juniors must acquire taste *on purpose*. Marshals the entry-level-erosion data (NY Fed graduate underemployment 41.5%; Indeed junior postings −34% since 2020; Stanford's 16% relative employment decline for 22–25s) against the optimistic net-growth story (WEF's +78M net roles by 2030), resolving the contradiction as "the net and the entry level are two different questions." Restates the **inner-loop (agent: investigate/implement/test/report) vs outer-loop (human: worth-attention? verify by evidence — diffs/tests/logs/why — approve or block, carry consequence)** boundary, names the **two debts (cognitive surrender + comprehension)**, and gives seven concrete habits — read more code than you generate, keep a "wrong log," specify and verify separately, **build an eval/rubric and run it on 50 real AI PRs** (the grader loop as personal calibration), calibrate autonomy per task.
Why it's worth reading: it's the human-side complement to the factory piece and ties `[[loop-engineering]]`, `[[willison-understand-to-participate]]`, and the spec-driven seam together; the "build a rubric, run it on 50 PRs" habit is a directly actionable verification-loop practice for your own agent workflows.
