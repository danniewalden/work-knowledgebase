## Read next

1. **deVadoss — CEAD: A Capability-Aligned Enterprise Agent Design** (arXiv 2605.08258, John deVadoss / InterWork Alliance) — `[[devadoss-cead-capability-aligned-agent-design]]` · new wiki page (07-31)
   - **TLDR:** A design-first reference architecture for multi-agent systems built around **durable business capabilities**, not agent roles or prompts. "Capability before agent; boundary before topology." Its core artifact is the **Agent Capability Contract (ACC)** — the agent-era descendant of Homann's capability black box and the SOA service contract, extended with 13 fields for autonomy, tools, memory, verification, and evaluation. A simulation over 10,000 enterprise tasks has CEAD at 70.6% "safe success" vs 45.2% mono-agent and 50.8% for a *governance-first-but-design-poor* grid — the headline being **governance cannot compensate for weak decomposition**, and ungoverned agent sprawl = a distributed monolith.
   - **Why worth reading:** This is the single most direct source tying your flagged-important **business-capabilities** thread to multi-agent architecture — it turns Homann → Goeleven → Fritzsche from a code/ownership pattern into the primary unit of agent design, with an empirical (if simulated) backbone.

2. **Skelton — Team Topologies as the Foundation for AI ROI** — `[[skelton-team-topologies-foundation-ai-roi]]` · new wiki page (07-31)

   - **Why worth reading:** Read it right after CEAD — together they close the loop **capability → team → agent**, extending your capabilities thread from an architecture pattern into an org-design one. Also the source of the standing "capture the DORA report" action below.

   - **Why worth reading:** The clearest signal on where the EM field is heading institutionally, and it reframes Johansen's "the constraint is adoption, not technique" as: *standardization is the adoption play*. Directly relevant to ChronosHub's public EM+ES+agentic strategy.
