## Read next

**1. "Thinking in Events" — Rico Fritzsche (ricofritzsche.me, 3 Jul 2026)** — `raw/articles/fritzsche-thinking-in-events.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
**TLDR:** Fritzsche argues most projects fail not from complexity but from translating a business situation into technical structure (tables, flags, status columns, generic `update` handlers) too early, losing the language that explains the domain. Event Modeling fixes this by describing the system as business facts unfolding over time — `ReservationCanceled` / `CheckInDateChanged` / `ReservationMarkedAsNoShow` instead of a lossy `ReservationUpdated`. His central claim: **thinking in events does NOT require Event Sourcing** — Event Modeling is a *modeling discipline*, Event Sourcing is a *storage decision*, and conflating them turns a good way of thinking into an unwanted technical prescription. He leans on Dymitruk ("events happen — whether we store them or not is our choice") and Fowler's strict ES definition to draw the boundary: ES only when the fact-history is worth being the authoritative state (audit, temporal reasoning, independent read models).
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the cleanest, most quotable statement of the EM-vs-ES separation you can hand to a leadership or non-ES audience — directly on your focus substrate, and the companion piece to the already-ingested [[fritzsche-choosing-storage-is-choosing-what-your-system-forgets]]. It's the one fresh unprocessed input in the KB right now.
