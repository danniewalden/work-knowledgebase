## Read next

**1. deVadoss — CEAD: A Capability-Aligned Enterprise Agent Design** *(arXiv 2605.08258; new wiki page + new entity `john-devadoss`)* — `[[devadoss-cead-capability-aligned-agent-design]]`
**TLDR.** A reference architecture that decomposes multi-agent systems around **durable business capabilities first, agents second** ("capability before agent; boundary before topology"). Its core artifact is the **Agent Capability Contract** — the SOA service contract reborn for the agent era, now carrying autonomy level, tool scopes, memory, verification and escalation. A 10,000-task simulation puts CEAD at **70.6% "safe success" vs 45.2%** mono-agent and **50.8%** for a *governance-first-but-design-poor* grid — i.e. strong governance still lost to good decomposition. Warning: ungoverned agent sprawl = a distributed monolith; systems degrade past 16–32 agents.
**Why it's worth reading.** This is the capstone of your flagged-important *business-capabilities* thread — the empirical, agent-era extension of Homann's capability black box, and it independently lands on Fritzsche's "capability is the home." Read it as the architectural spine for how many agents you should have and where their boundaries go.

**2. Skelton — Why Team Topologies Is the Essential Foundation for AI ROI** *(teamtopologies.com; new wiki page + new entity `matthew-skelton`)* — `[[skelton-team-topologies-foundation-ai-roi]]`
**TLDR.** Reads the 2026 DORA ROI report, whose finding is that **AI is an amplifier** — it magnifies a healthy org's strengths and a dysfunctional org's debt equally; tokens guarantee nothing. The argument: **value-flow-aligned team boundaries are the boundary that makes an autonomous agent effective** (a siloed org can't even define value, so its agents "generate code that lacks business context"). It names the **verification tax / J-curve** and the fix (push verification into enabling/platform teams as a service), and calls for **data-as-a-product "digestible for humans and agents."**
**Why it's worth reading.** The organization-side mirror of CEAD's architecture-side claim — together they close the loop **capability → team → agent**. Directly relevant to ChronosHub's EM+ES+agentic strategy: it's the "how do we structure teams so agents pay off" half.

**3. jwilger/agent-skills — the event model as the spec that drives the factory** *(new wiki page)* — `[[jwilger-agent-skills-factory-pipeline]]`
**TLDR.** A second, sharper capture of jwilger's repo read for one claim: **direction of fit reverses.** Most EM×agent sources point *into* the model (an agent authors it, or is modeled as a processor); here humans write the event model **first**, and its output is the **contract a whole factory of coding agents is measured against**. v4.1 wires the model literally into execution: each slice carries an `event_model_root` path; a slice **missing a GWT scenario with a boundary is rejected**; an incomplete event model **blocks decomposition**; GWT gates + mutation + CI + git-worktree isolation replace human approval as autonomy climbs Conservative→Standard→Full.
**Why it's worth reading.** The cleanest worked instance of "EM is the human-authored spec at the top of the autonomy ladder" and the load-bearing evidence for GWT-as-machine-checkable-acceptance-gate — the exact seam of `[[event-modeled-agent-design]]`.

**4. Roden — "Too Many Islands, Too Few Bridges" (EM Conference 2026 recap)** *(the native web blog; new wiki page + new concept `em-standardization-foundation`)* — `[[roden-too-many-islands-em-conf-2026]]`
**TLDR.** The long-missing published recap of the 2nd EM Conference (Munich, Jun 25–26). Its defining theme is that the ES/CQRS/DDD world is **"an archipelago"** — fragmented by methodology (aggregates vs DCB), vocabulary (event vs domain event, read model vs projection), canonical examples, and tooling. Day two produced **"The Munich Event"**: a signed flip-chart commitment to a neutral standards foundation (CNCF spirit). Roden's own talk — *"Your Domain Model Belongs in the Repository"* — argued the model must live as versioned plain text in Git, and demoed ESDM round-tripping **code→model→code** live with an AI (reverse-engineer a model, then generate Kotlin + Python from it).
**Why it's worth reading.** Reframes Johansen's "the constraint is adoption, not technique" as: **standardization *is* the adoption play** — and gives you the organizer-side view of where the EM community is heading. The AI code↔model↔code demo is a live instance of your focus edge.

**5. Prefect (Lowin) — Loops vs. graphs (directed agentic graphs)** *(FastMCP podcast; new wiki page + new concept `graph-engineering`)* — `[[prefect-loops-vs-graphs]]`
**TLDR.** The authored primary for **loops(micro) vs graphs(macro)**: a loop is one agent's internal behaviour; a **directed agentic graph** is orchestration *across* agents, where a node is an agent-in-a-harness (its own tools/model/access/skills) and an edge returns control to you after a decision. Lowin rebrands DAG (drops "acyclic", keeps "directed"), and makes the security case the headline: don't hand an agent the refund tool plus a polite skill note — split into a graph where diligence happens in a node that **cannot** issue refunds, and only a programmatic control-return grants node 2 a **single-use, one-customer** refund tool. "95% things we've done for decades, 5% new sparkles."
**Why it's worth reading.** Names the macro layer above loop engineering and gives a concrete governance mechanism (node-level capability scoping) that rhymes with your guardian-agent / DCB-guard worked models — the "org chart made of loops" drawn as an actual control-flow diagram.

**6. Dilger — Flea-market registration, model→code→deploy in 30 min** *(LinkedIn; `vibe-modeling` grown stub→full page)* — `[[dilger-flea-market-model-to-deploy]]`
**TLDR.** A non-technical stakeholder (his wife) co-models a flea-market signup by **drawing screens**, without ever being told what Event Modeling is — model→code→deploy in under 30 minutes because the tooling pre-made the architecture decisions. Honest boundary: he concedes Claude could have built *this* trivial app with no model, but "the more complex a system gets, the more that planning step earns its keep."
**Why it's worth reading.** The KB's strongest concrete instance of `[[vibe-modeling]]` — the front-end where a non-developer co-designs before any code — and a rare non-dogmatic statement of *when* modeling pays off. Short, illustrative, worth 5 minutes.
