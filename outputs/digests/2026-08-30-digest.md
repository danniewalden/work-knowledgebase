### 1. "Last night, I spent 1 Million+ Tokens to train my Modeling Agent" — Martin Dilger (LinkedIn, 2026-08-27)
`raw/notes/dilger-one-million-tokens-self-training-modeling-agent.md` — *filed, not yet ingested*

**TLDR.** Dilger built a hill-climbing loop that improves his Event Modeling agent without him in the seat. The grader is a corpus of Event Models he considers well-crafted: the agent models a realistic requirement set, structurally diffs its output against the good corpus (chapter layout, read-model structure, given/when/then shape, pattern use), edits *its own skills*, then re-models and compares against both the corpus and its previous attempt. Three rules it discovered and wrote back into the skills: use storylines rather than plain GWTs for read models attached to automations; use linked elements to bridge events across chapters; use read-model copies instead of back arrows. It runs continuously on local hardware (QWEN3.7:27b).

**Why it's worth reading.** This is the best EM×agents primary since the GOTO talk, and it is the seam your Topic-4 watch exists to catch: Event Modeling used as the *verification rubric* for a self-improving agent. It is the strongest evidence yet that slice/GWT structure is what makes an agent's output gradeable at all — which is the load-bearing claim under your whole chspec direction.

### 3. "Making Your Data Ready for Agentic AI" — Pramod Sadalage & Prem Chandrasekaran, Thoughtworks (martinfowler.com, 2026-08-27)
`raw/articles/sadalage-chandrasekaran-making-data-ready-for-agentic-ai.md` — *filed, not yet ingested* (~10k words, the longest capture in the window)

**TLDR.** The argument starts from one asymmetry: "a human hesitates at data that looks wrong; an agent acts on it anyway" — so everything a human analyst supplies implicitly (what "revenue" means here, which tables to avoid, that this total looks suspiciously round) has to be made explicit in three layers: a trusted data foundation, a context/semantic layer, and an access layer that controls how agents act. The pieces that matter for your thread: a **capability model** where every capability declares permissions, an owner, **preconditions checked against live state at the moment of acting**, and a **reversibility class** — with the claim that *reversibility predicts safe autonomy better than transaction size*; **"agentic lineage"** (traces that record *why*, not just *what*, keyed to EU AI Act Art. 12/19); **"retrieved text informs, it never gates"** as a concrete structural answer to prompt injection; "design capabilities, not endpoints" with a Radar HOLD on naive API-to-MCP conversion; and the AtScale datum that text-to-SQL accuracy went from <20% on a raw schema to >92.5% with a semantic layer, same model.

**Why it's worth reading.** This is your **top-priority focus area** — business capabilities — treated seriously by a non-vendor primary, and it is a data-architecture sibling of Devadoss's Agent Capability Contract arriving independently. It is also the KB's first substantial treatment of the data/context substrate *underneath* agents, so it is hub-forming: several thin concept pages get real grounding out of one ingest.

### 4. "Slicing keeps the cost curve flat — Triplet Architecture" — Martin Dilger (LinkedIn, 2026-08-29)
`raw/notes/dilger-slicing-keeps-the-cost-curve-flat-triplet-architecture.md` — *filed, not yet ingested*

**TLDR.** Architecture restated as a single economic claim: "building a feature in 5 years costs exactly as much or less as today. That's the only purpose of architecture." Friction accumulates as coupling; that's why AI struggles in grown codebases, and "attempting Spec-Driven-Development without having a solution to this is like playing russian roulette." His fix is consistent slicing decided *during specification* — "Slices are like Candy for AI" — and he names the combination of Event Modeling + Event Sourcing + slicing **"Triplet-Architecture"**.

**Why it's worth reading.** It's the clearest statement yet of *why* slicing is the load-bearing move for agents (token economics of traversal, not aesthetics) — and it rhymes with Jeremy Miller's independent "layered architectures force AI agents to burn more tokens" line, from a practitioner who disagrees with Dilger about almost everything else. It also names a methodology the KB has no page for, and points at an uncaptured article behind an `lnkd.in` shortlink.

### 5. "Agentic Engineering at Zalando: a snapshot" — Bartosz Ocytko (Zalando Engineering Blog, 2026-08-14)
`raw/articles/zalando-agentic-engineering-snapshot.md` — *filed, not yet ingested*

**TLDR.** A rare non-vendor production account at scale — 250+ engineering teams over 2.5 years. The numbers: a risk-based bot auto-approves **33% of PRs** using rules derived from their own outage post-mortems, cutting PR lead time **20–40%**; PR sizes climbing since Sonnet-4, with agent-written commit messages clustering around 5k characters; a four-codebase cyclomatic-complexity study that pinpoints the inflection point where coding agents enter, with the agentic-from-day-0 codebase spiking fastest. Also a company-wide agent-skill marketplace (migration skills most popular), an internal LiteLLM proxy with MCP token injection so no secrets sit in config files, and a deliberate refusal to standardise on one tool ("it's way too early"). Two lines to quote: "AI amplifies the good and bad practices across our organization" and "using coding agents usually inhibits learning".

**Why it's worth reading.** Your best independent production evidence since Stripe's minions, and the only capture in the window with real organizational numbers rather than a practitioner's assertion. The complexity-inflection finding is the empirical shape of comprehension debt.
