## Read next

1. **Loop Engineering — Addy Osmani** (addyosmani.com, 2026-06-07) · `raw/articles/addyosmani-loop-engineering.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
   **TLDR:** The definitional primary for "loop engineering": you stop being the person who prompts the agent and instead **design the system that prompts it**. Osmani places it "one floor above the harness" — a harness that runs on a timer, spawns helpers, and feeds itself. He decomposes a loop into five primitives plus memory: **automations** (scheduled discovery/triage, `/loop` and `/goal`), **worktrees** (parallel agents without collisions), **skills** (`SKILL.md` project knowledge so the loop doesn't re-derive context every cycle), **plugins/connectors** (MCP into your real tools), and **sub-agents** (split the maker from the checker) — over an on-disk **state file** (markdown or Linear) because the model forgets between runs. Quotes Steinberger ("design loops that prompt your agents") and Boris Cherny ("I write loops"), and is candid about the three risks that get *sharper* as loops improve: verification, comprehension debt, and cognitive surrender.
   **Why it's worth reading:** This is the conceptual keystone that reframes your whole harness/ralph-loop thread one level up — and its five-primitive anatomy maps almost exactly onto Dilger's **Event Modeling Agent Harness** (24/7 loop, slice-as-unit-of-work, GWT verification), giving you the general pattern that Dilger's EM-specific version instantiates.

2. **The Art of Loop Engineering — Sydney Runkle / LangChain** (langchain.com, 2026-06-16) · `raw/articles/langchain-the-art-of-loop-engineering.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
   **TLDR:** The same idea rendered as a concrete **4-loop stack** (after swyx's "loopcraft"): (1) the **agent loop** — model calls tools until done; (2) the **verification loop** — a grader (deterministic or LLM-as-judge) scores output against a rubric and retries with feedback; (3) the **event-driven loop** — cron/webhook/channel triggers make the agent always-on inside your ecosystem; (4) the **hill-climbing loop** — an analysis agent reads run traces and *rewrites the harness config itself* (prompts, tools, graders), so each outer cycle makes the inner loops better. Stresses maker≠checker and human-in-the-loop touchpoints at every level. Vendor-framed (maps each loop to a LangChain primitive), but the taxonomy is clean and the hill-climbing loop is the genuinely new idea.
   **Why it's worth reading:** Gives you the verification + self-improvement structure your harness-engineering pages currently lack a vocabulary for — Loop 4 (trace-driven harness rewriting) is the missing "feedback that improves the controls, not just the code," directly extensible to an EM-governed agent factory where the event model is the rubric.

3. **"You just cannot trust your coding agent to produce maintainable code" — Adam Tornhill** (LinkedIn, 2026-06-22) · `raw/notes/tornhill-cannot-trust-agent-maintainable-code-codescene-mcp.md` · *filed, not yet ingested*
   **TLDR:** A short, pointed field note: Tornhill (coding 100% agentic for 9 months) told an agent to fix a code-health issue and it traded one problem for a worse one. His argument: asking an agent to "follow SOLID" / "write clean code" or having another LLM review it is **not enough** — LLMs are non-deterministic, can't reliably assess code health, and the messy existing code *is* the context, so agents do worst exactly where they're needed most. His fix is **deterministic Code Health feedback via the CodeScene MCP** as the agent's external source of truth rather than self-judgment.
   **Why it's worth reading:** Sharpens your code-health × agentic-coding thread (the Borg/Tornhill FORGE paper, CLEAR, ai-readable-code) into a harness prescription — and is a perfect concrete instance of LangChain's "verification loop" with a *deterministic, external* grader, tying #2 and #3 together.
