## Read next

1. **Command Context Consistency — new concept page** · `[[command-context-consistency]]` · *new wiki page (substrate, focus)*
   **TLDR:** CCC = record a command's outcome *only while the facts its decision read still hold*. It replaces the fixed aggregate (a "Static Consistency Boundary" that is simultaneously too large — unrelated edits to one reservation falsely collide on a shared version — and too small — cross-entity invariants live outside any aggregate) with a boundary the command draws for itself: read → decide → record, where `record` carries the condition. Store-agnostic: a relational conditional `UPDATE…WHERE` + `UNIQUE` constraint, or an event-store conditional append — the DCB is just this guard in tag-based form.
   **Why it's worth reading:** This is the page that finally unifies the KB's scattered CCC/DCB/aggregate material into one spine (Westphal named it, Fritzsche derived it, Pellegrini's DCB is one form of it) — the single clearest statement of the write-side consistency model under your whole EM/event-sourcing substrate.

2. **Fritzsche — Who Owns a Rule Shared Across Domain Capabilities?** · `[[fritzsche-who-owns-a-rule-shared-across-domain-capabilities]]` · *new source (business-capabilities, focus — flagged important)*
   **TLDR:** The most direct answer yet to the objection "doesn't capability autonomy fall apart on shared rules like identity, money, compliance?" Fritzsche's move: stop treating them as one "shared invariants" bucket and split by architectural home — state invariant (enforced *at commit*, not by a shared helper), external fact (a Provider), stable semantics (a small pure type), shared policy (one versioned authority + a conformance suite run against each local evaluator), capability decision (the owning RPU). "One policy, many local evaluators"; shared *code* ≠ shared *knowledge*; `GenericService.isAllowed()` is the anti-pattern because it hides the facts that explain the answer.
   **Why it's worth reading:** Directly advances your flagged-important business-capabilities thread — it closes the strongest standing objection to capability-as-boundary and gives you concrete, defensible language for where a shared rule actually lives.

3. **Fritzsche — Why SOLID Is Outdated** · `[[fritzsche-why-solid-is-outdated]]` · *new source (ai-readable-code, focus)*
   **TLDR:** "I recommended SOLID for years. I was wrong." SOLID was priced for a dead world (hour-long compiles, risky releases); four of its five principles give *no failure signal* when they're damaging the design (every extra split/interface/extension-point counts as "compliance"), OCP is an admitted bet on prescience, and "one reason to change" decides nothing. Replacement: North's CUPID, held by degree. The agent-era hook: **coding agents reproduce SOLID by default unless the repository's design policy tells them otherwise.**
   **Why it's worth reading:** A strong new voice converging with Tornhill's CLEAR and Khononov on your ai-readable-code thread — and it reframes design principles as a *harness* concern (the repo's conventions are the guide the agent inherits), which is exactly your focus intersection.

4. **Dilger — Spec-Driven tools need Event Modeling as their front half** · `[[dilger-spec-driven-tools-need-event-modeling-front-half]]` · *new source (EM×agents, focus)*
   **TLDR:** Dilger fed deliberately-vague requirements to Spec Kitty; by its third question it was already asking about the tech stack and it emitted a premature "domain model," then **46 markdown files after 20 minutes** — "skipping the visual model doesn't remove the complexity, it just removes the thing that was managing it." His resolution: Event Modeling is the problem-understanding *front half* that feeds SDD toolkits (Kiro, Spec-Kit, Spec-Kitty), shipped as a standardized CLI bridge — `eventmodelers export --spec-kitty`. "The tools aren't the problem. Skipping the digging is."
   **Why it's worth reading:** A genuinely new EM×agents interop seam (EM → SDD task lists) and a concrete instance of your recurring "the hard 80% is requirements" thesis — useful if ChronosHub is weighing how EM sits alongside the SDD tooling wave.

5. **DORA — ROI of AI-Assisted Software Development (2026.01)** · `[[dora-roi-ai-assisted-software-development-2026]]` · *new source (loop/governance, focus)*
   **TLDR:** DORA's ROI report frames AI as an **amplifier of the organizational system** (platform quality, workflow clarity, team alignment) rather than a tool you buy returns from, and names a **J-curve**: a temporary productivity dip from the learning curve + verification tax + downstream adaptation ("the tuition cost of transformation" — don't pull funding during the dip). ROI flows AI → 7 capabilities → DORA metrics → DevEx → cost/revenue; an illustrative 500-eng org shows ~39% first-year ROI, ~8-month payback (self-labelled high-uncertainty). Also: an instability tax (change-fail 5%→6%) and Stanford's 35–40% greenfield vs ≤10% legacy gain.
   **Why it's worth reading:** It grounds the Skelton "Team Topologies as the foundation for AI ROI" source with hard framing and gives you the amplifier / J-curve / instability-tax vocabulary — the environment-over-model through-line, now with numbers you can quote to leadership.
