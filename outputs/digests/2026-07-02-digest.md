**1. Dilger — "My Wife Asked Me to Build a Registration Form for a Flea Market"** — Martin Dilger, LinkedIn 2026-07-01
*Path:* `raw/notes/dilger-flea-market-model-code-deploy-30min.md` — **filed, not yet ingested (newest capture)**
**TLDR:** A real anecdote: Dilger opens the Eventmodelers platform and, just by drawing the screens *with* his non-technical wife (who "has no idea what Event Modeling is and doesn't care"), brainstorms → models → builds → deploys a working flea-market registration app — including a Stripe/Copecart payment option and a "filter who hasn't paid" button — in under 30 minutes. His three takeaways: the method should be *invisible tooling* that solves the user's problem; "Could Claude have done this without any modeling? Sure — here it wouldn't have mattered. But the more complex a system gets, the more that planning step earns its keep"; and event sourcing is "actually simpler than the alternatives once you're used to it," not a dogma. It's fast because project setup, event-store choice, and architecture are pre-decided by the platform — "one button click away from Model → Code → Deploy → rinse repeat."
**Why it's worth reading:** Tight EM×agents focus — a concrete, non-expert-modeler datapoint for the eventmodelers.ai / build-kits / model-is-a-living-spec thread, and it sharpens Dilger's recurring "planning earns its keep as complexity grows" line (ties directly to his `dilger-harness-is-20-percent-requirements-are-80`).

**2. Dilger — "We need the model in the code": draw.io, AI manipulation, MCP guardrails** — Martin Dilger, LinkedIn 2026-06-30
*Path:* `raw/notes/dilger-drawio-event-modeling-model-in-code.md` — **filed, not yet ingested (carried over)**
**TLDR:** Dilger endorses keeping the event model *in the repo* and letting an agent manipulate it, but argues raw draw.io XML gives the agent no rules to follow. **Why it's worth reading:** The most concrete "model-in-code + agent + MCP-as-guardrail" workflow Dilger has posted; directly extends `dilger-event-modeling-agent-harness` and the EmLang / model-as-source-of-truth thread.

**3. Dilger — first-ever extension of the Event Modeling "standard": an optional "Query" WHEN** — Martin Dilger, LinkedIn 2026-06-29
*Path:* `raw/notes/dilger-extending-event-modeling-query-when.md` — **filed, not yet ingested (carried over)**
**TLDR:** Dilger, who says he'd deliberately never deviated from Dymitruk's EM standard, adds one element: an *optional* `WHEN` ("Query") on the **read side** of Given-When-Then, so read-model scenarios can express complex queries (GIVEN a user was registered / WHEN we query by email / THEN the user is returned). Driven by repeated independent requests at the Munich EM Conference — framed as feedback-driven, not unilateral.
**Why it's worth reading:** A rare change to the EM notation itself, touching `given-when-then` — the acceptance-gate seam for agent-built slices. Worth knowing before it propagates into tooling and the course.

**4. Enzler — Event Sourcing: Aggregates, DCB, or what?** — Urs Enzler, planetgeek.ch 2026-06-23
*Path:* `[[enzler-event-sourcing-aggregates-dcb-or-what]]` — **new wiki page (just ingested)**
**TLDR:** Part 11 of Enzler's ES series proposes a pragmatic **third way** between DDD aggregates ("can quickly lead to overly large aggregates") and DCBs ("powerful, but quite complicated"): *design the concurrency away.* Concretely — most data has no real concurrent changes; use "small data" + a **task-based UI** (one command per task, no unit-of-work); where a real invariant is at stake, **replace a concurrent design with a non-concurrent one** (the classic course-registration case becomes draft-intent + a single-threaded batch that also enables prioritisation); and serialise only where truly needed via infrastructure (Azure Service Bus **sessions**), not a domain construct.
**Why it's worth reading:** Adds a genuinely new axis to the KB's DCB debate (DCB removes the boundary / conventional view shrinks the aggregate / Enzler removes the *concurrency*), from a new independent practitioner voice — and his Service-Bus-sessions answer is the infrastructure analogue of Fritzsche's serialised-write-order point one layer down. Substrate only; no agents angle developed.
