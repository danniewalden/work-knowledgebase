1. **Simon Willison — "Understand to participate"** (simonwillison.net, 2026-07-02) — *filed, not yet ingested* (`raw/articles/willison-understand-to-participate`)
   - **TLDR:** Willison relays Geoffrey Litt's AIE-talk framing: as coding agents build larger, more sophisticated changes, you have to *understand the code deeply enough to keep participating* in the creative process, or you accrue **cognitive/comprehension debt** as your mental model drifts from what the code actually does. You need "a rich set of concepts in your mind to think fluently about how to move something forward" — without that fluency, your ability to steer the work is meaningfully limited.
   - **Why it's worth reading:** It's the crisp, quotable statement of the comprehension-debt seam that runs through your design-substrate focus — the human-side counterpart to Tornhill's AI-readable-code and Böckeler's context-engineering, and the "stay the engineer" caveat in loop engineering. A one-page read that sharpens *why* legible, model-first code matters.

Plus practitioner notes: review the doc edits first to understand a change; release notes are ideal agent-outsourceable work; harder tasks parallelize better (10–15 min churn); and a rare concrete cost datapoint (~$149.25 across main session + 4 subagents).
   - **Why it's worth reading:** The most concrete in-the-wild evidence yet for the loop-engineering grader/reflection loops (LangChain, PRINCE) and Willison's own "designing agentic loops" — with an actual dollar figure and a cross-model-review pattern you could adopt.
