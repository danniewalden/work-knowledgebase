1. **Fritzsche — The Command Context Consistency Principle** (Rico Fritzsche, ricofritzsche.me, 2026-07-26) — `[[fritzsche-command-context-consistency-principle]]` · *new wiki page, ingested 08-03*
   - **TLDR:** The canonical statement of CCC: *"record the outcome of a command only while the facts its decision read still hold."* The aggregate is the wrong unit — it's a *static* boundary that's simultaneously too large (unrelated edits falsely conflict) and too small (cross-aggregate invariants escape it). The real boundary is the exact set of facts a command reads, which changes per command. The rule is store-agnostic: an event store enforces it with a conditional append over a query; a relational DB with `UPDATE … WHERE`/`FOR SHARE`/`UNIQUE`. A failed guard is a *business outcome*, not a technical error.
   - **Why it's worth reading:** This is the cleanest resolution of the DCB-vs-aggregate debate the KB has been circling for weeks — CCC is the representation-agnostic principle, DCB its tag-based event-store form — and it lands directly on your business-capabilities focus: the capability's RPU carries the read-set into the commit condition, which is also the GWT GIVEN clause.
