## Read next

1. **Autonomous Domain Capabilities & Command Context Consistency** — Rico Fritzsche (LinkedIn, 2026-06-08). *`raw/notes/rico-fritzsche-autonomous-domain-capabilities-ccc.md` — filed, not yet ingested.*
   **TLDR:** Argues AI coding agents' speed exposes an old weakness — most architectures give a domain capability no real "home." Layered/Clean/Hexagonal split one capability across technical layers; even Vertical Slice leaks ownership when the handler still leans on shared domain models, repositories, or aggregates. His fix: stop routing Commands/Queries through a centralized object model. Each capability is a self-contained **Request Processing Unit** that defines the facts it needs, builds a *local* context from recorded events, decides, and emits consequences — so behavior, ownership, and change stay local. The domain = recorded state + the capabilities that interpret it.
   **Why it's worth reading:** Hits your flagged-important **business-capabilities** focus head-on and ties it to VSA, event-sourcing, *and* agent ownership in one frame — the clearest "why capabilities are the agent's blast-radius" argument yet. (Full version is a Medium article linked in his first comment — not captured; worth grabbing if you want the depth.)

2. **Business Capabilities** (concept page) — `wiki/concepts/business-capabilities.md`. *New/updated wiki page, tagged `focus`.*
   **TLDR:** The KB's synthesized hub for the flagged-important topic, now grounded on the Homann 2006 primary: a capability is the stable *what* (not *how*), a measurable black box with a service-level contract, organized as a nested taxonomy and linked by "capability connectors." Explains why capabilities make good boundaries (coupling/cohesion: the "what" is stable, the "how" churns), maps them onto DDD bounded contexts / Conway / OCP / VSA, carries Goeleven's capability-swimlane Event Modeling and his "contract between capabilities" rule, and frames the capabilities × Wardley strategy lens.
   **Why it's worth reading:** It's the one page that pulls your whole flagged focus together and gives the agent-ownership tie-in — read it right after Rico's note to see the primary-sourced version of the same argument.

3. **AI was trained on dysfunction — so agents are a must** — Adam Dymitruk (LinkedIn, 2026-06-15). *`raw/notes/dymitruk-ai-trained-on-dysfunction-agents-are-a-must.md` — filed, not yet ingested.*

Explicitly points to Martin Dilger and Yordis Prieto as the way to get there.
   **Why it's worth reading:** The creator of Event Modeling stating the *why* for the EM×agents thesis in one line, and naming Yordis Prieto as a new voice to potentially add to the watch — directly on your tightest focus.

4. **The Codebase Is the Prompt: Wolverine, Vertical Slices, and AI-Assisted Development** — Jeremy D. Miller, The Shade Tree Developer (2026-06-04). *`raw/articles/miller-codebase-is-the-prompt-vertical-slices-ai.md` — filed, not yet ingested.*
   **TLDR:** Makes the locality-of-reference case concretely: layered architectures force an agent to load 6–7 scattered files to change one behavior, collapsing signal-to-noise and triggering hallucination, whereas vertical slices keep a feature in one place. Then pushes further — *small* slices beat merely co-located ones — showing how Wolverine compresses a slice to almost pure business decision (no marker interfaces, method injection, `[Transactional]`, cascading-message returns, aggregate handlers). The honest caveat: compression shifts the "how it wires together" burden, which is why conventions must be encoded as agent skill files ("the skills are the constitution; the slices are the code").
   **Why it's worth reading:** The most worked, code-level treatment of VSA-as-AI-substrate in the KB, and a candidate bridge between vertical-slice-architecture and the harness/agent-legibility thread; note the vendor angle (it's partly a Critter Stack pitch).

5. **DCB is event sourcing what it always should have been** — Martin Dilger (LinkedIn, 2026-06-15). *`raw/notes/dilger-dcb-is-what-event-sourcing-should-have-been.md` — filed, not yet ingested.*
   **TLDR:** A naming provocation: "Dynamic Consistency Boundaries" is a clumsy term for what should just be called "event sourcing," and the *aggregate*-based approach people struggle with is what deserved a qualifier ("Static Consistency Boundaries"). Notes DCB gets a prominent place in the 2nd edition of his book *Understanding Eventsourcing*.
   **Why it's worth reading:** A one-paragraph read that frames the DCB-vs-aggregate debate on your substrate thread and flags where the canonical treatment is headed; pairs with the pellegrini-dcb seed already ingested.
