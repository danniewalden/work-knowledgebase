**1. Oskar Dudycz — "Announcing Strictland: contract testing for message compatibility"** *(Event-Driven.io, 2026-06-15)*
`raw/articles/dudycz-strictland-contract-testing-message-compatibility.md` — **filed 06-18, NOT yet ingested** (oldest unprocessed input; on-thread).
**TLDR:** Dudycz released **Strictland**, a JVM (Java/Scala/Kotlin) open-source contract-testing library for **message/event schema compatibility**. You write a spec — `given(new OrderPlaced(...)).whenDeserializedAs(OrderPlacedWithCoupon.class).thenBackwardCompatible()` — that asserts an old event still deserializes into a new shape (and `thenForwardCompatible` the reverse). The approach is snapshot-file-in-repo: no broker, no schema registry, the compatibility contract lives in your test suite.
**Why it's worth reading:** This is a concrete tool for the exact problem [[event-versioning-and-upcasting]] keeps raising in your substrate thread (the "avoid upcasters" vs "version events early" debate) — a way to *test* event-schema evolution rather than argue about it. It's the most on-thread of the un-ingested backlog and the cheapest to file.

**2. Vlad Khononov — "Modularity Skills" Claude Code plugin** *(vladikk on GitHub)*
[[khononov-modularity-claude-code-plugin]] — **new wiki page (ingested 06-22).**
**TLDR:** Khononov shipped his Balanced Coupling model as a runnable plugin with two skills: `/modularity:review` audits an existing codebase for coupling imbalances across integration-strength/distance/volatility and emits an MD+HTML review with coupling.dev links; `/modularity:high-level-design` does the inverse — designs a modular architecture from requirements with per-module integration contracts + test specs, self-reviewing until clean. Framing: AI generates code faster, so misdrawn boundaries and tech debt compound faster — so operate at the architectural level, not the line level.
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the coupling-substrate analogue of the EM-as-agent-skill pattern you track (jwilger, prooph board) — "turn a design discipline into a harness skill" — and the self-reviewing design loop is a guides-and-sensors harness in miniature. Sits squarely on your business-capabilities/coupling × agents thread.

**3. Oskar Dudycz — "You can fork a package, but can you own it?"** *(Event-Driven.io, 2026-06-08)*
`raw/articles/dudycz-fork-a-package-can-you-own-it.md` — **filed 06-18, NOT yet ingested** (flagged off-thread).
**TLDR:** Riffing on a Mitchell Hashimoto post ("fork your dependencies, trim them, don't update unless it breaks"), Dudycz argues the real issue isn't forking but *ownership* — most teams "take a dependency" by installing one without ever *deciding* to own it, and that undecided move is the root of supply-chain attacks, licence dramas, and half-finished SBOMs. The on-thread hook is his **"LLM as a fork"** section: LLMs change the cost of *producing* code, not *owning* it; "vibe it and move on" is the same skipped decision as "install and move on."
**Why it's worth reading:** Mostly supply-chain/architecture (off your core focus), but the "LLM as a fork / who owns vibe-coded code" angle is a sharp responsibility argument for the [[agentic-coding]] / harness thread. Worth a skim to decide whether to ingest just that section or drop it.
