## Read next

**1. Addy Osmani — "Earning taste and judgment" (AddyOsmani.com, 2026-07-14)**
`raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment.md` — *filed 07-21, not yet ingested*

- **TLDR:** Taste and judgment used to be a byproduct of doing thousands of "reps" (boilerplate, bug fixes); agents now take the reps, so juniors must acquire judgment deliberately. Osmani marshals 2026 labor data (elevated recent-grad unemployment, tech postings shifting toward 5+ years' experience, Stanford's revised −16% employment for the most AI-exposed 22–25s) against the optimistic net-jobs story, then argues the durable, "ungradeable" human contribution is *choosing what to build and judging whether it's any good.* Gives seven concrete habits (read more code than you generate, keep a "wrong log," specify and verify separately, build a rubric-based eval and run it on 50 real AI PRs, calibrate autonomy per task) and four principles (finish the last mile, solve the hard version, build in public near hard problems, be a T-shaped generalist).
- **Why it's worth reading:** It's the human-accountability capstone of your loop-engineering thread — it operationalizes "stay the engineer" (Osmani's own [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]] and Willison's [[willison-directly-responsible-individuals]]) into a personal practice, and its "build an eval / rubric, run on 50 PRs" and "specify and verify separately" both land directly on the grader-loop and spec-driven seams you care about.

**2. arXiv 2604.25850 — "Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses" (Fudan/Peking, Apr 2026)**
*referenced in the watch log; not yet in `raw/` — capture candidate, out-of-window*

- **TLDR:** A closed-loop system where an agent **autonomously evolves its own harness** via three observability pillars (component / experience / decision), with each edit framed as a falsifiable prediction. Ten iterations lift Terminal-Bench-2 pass@1 from 69.7→77.0% (beating human-designed Codex-CLI at 71.9% and ACE/TF-GRPO baselines) and transfer to SWE-bench-verified at 75.6% with −12% tokens.
- **Why it's worth reading:** It is the empirical realization of LangChain's fourth "hill-climbing" loop ([[langchain-the-art-of-loop-engineering]]) — the self-improving-harness idea your KB currently supports only with vendor/practitioner framing. This is the peer-review-grade anchor the loop-engineering thread has been missing; worth capturing even though it predates the rolling window.
