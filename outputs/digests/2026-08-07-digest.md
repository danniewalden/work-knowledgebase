1. **deVadoss — CEAD: A Capability-Aligned Enterprise Agent Design** (John deVadoss, InterWork Alliance/IEEE; arXiv:2605.08258, 2026-05-07) — `[[devadoss-cead-capability-aligned-agent-design]]` · *wiki page, ingested late-July*
   - **TLDR:** The KB's most direct source tying **business capabilities to multi-agent architecture**. Its rule is *"capability before agent, boundary before topology"* — define the durable business capability and its owner *before* naming an agent role or prompt, and decompose only where there's a durable reason (separate ownership, materially different tool authority, distinct data classification, specialized evaluation, independent release cadence). The core design artifact is the **Agent Capability Contract (ACC)** — the agent-era descendant of Homann's contracted black box and the SOA service contract, now carrying autonomy, memory, and verification (13 fields). A 10,000-task simulation has CEAD at **70.6% "safe success" vs a governance-first-but-design-poor grid at 50.8%** — i.e. *governance cannot compensate for weak decomposition* — and ungoverned agent swarms degrade sharply past 16–32 agents ("agent sprawl = a distributed monolith").
   - **Why it's worth reading:** This is the flagged-important business-capabilities thread stated as an *agent architecture*: it independently arrives at Fritzsche's capability-as-home argument and Homann's black-box contract, and gives you a concrete artifact (the ACC) and a design discipline for the exact question ChronosHub's EM+ES+agentic strategy has to answer — how many agents, owned by what. Caveat: the eval is a parameterized simulation, not a field study.

2. **Fritzsche — Who Owns a Rule Shared Across Domain Capabilities?** (Rico Fritzsche, ricofritzsche.me, 2026-07-30) — `[[fritzsche-who-owns-a-rule-shared-across-domain-capabilities]]` · *wiki page, ingested 08-03*
   - **TLDR:** The most direct answer yet to the standing objection *"doesn't capability autonomy break down on shared rules like identity, money, and compliance?"* Fritzsche's move is to stop treating "shared invariants" as one bucket and split them by **architectural home**: a *state invariant* is enforced at the commit boundary (not by a shared helper); an *external fact* lives in a Provider; *stable semantics* (money = amount + currency) is a small pure type; a *shared policy* (e.g. AML-42) gets **one authoritative versioned release with a conformance suite**, distributed to each capability as local code — "one policy, many local evaluators" — while the **final decision always stays with the owning capability**. `GenericService.isAllowed()` is the anti-pattern because it hides the facts that explain the answer.
   - **Why it's worth reading:** It closes the biggest theoretical hole in the autonomous-capabilities argument you've been tracking, and it's immediately practical — it tells you *where* a cross-cutting rule physically lives without rebuilding a central service layer, and grounds it in CCC's commit-time enforcement. Caveat: single-author practitioner primary; the policy-authority machinery is described, not measured.

3. **Dilger — The Triplet: a flexible, agent-enabled architecture** (Martin Dilger, *Event Modeling applied* newsletter, 2026-07-26) — `[[dilger-triplet-flexible-agent-enabled-architecture]]` · *wiki page — and the full version of yesterday's Hold-my-Beer stub*
   - **TLDR:** Dilger's definitive naming of **"the Triplet"** — Event Modeling (plan) + Vertical Slices (structure) + Event Sourcing (store), used *in union*. The argument: the root problem is coupling, and layered/hexagonal architectures assume you already know your boundaries without helping you find them, so the boundary gets decided "by whoever was loudest that day." Event Modeling finds the boundary with stakeholders; slices are the natural structural result; event sourcing decouples the slices underneath. The **agent seam**: low coupling makes a slice cheap for an AI agent to build — "an agent working on one slice only needs that slice's context, the event log, and a precise model instead of half the codebase," so adding agents makes the system *done faster, not slower*. Includes Slice-Cycle-Time as his estimation alternative to story points.
   - **Why it's worth reading:** It's the single clearest statement of the EM×agents operating model the KB tracks, and it packages exactly the substrate ChronosHub is betting on. Bonus: it resolves the long-standing "Hold my Beer" stub yesterday's digest flagged — this *is* the full article behind that teaser. Caveat: vendor-authored (promotes his platform/book), self-reported, no external quality data.

4. **Worked Event Model — An Autonomous Customer-Support Desk** (in-house deliverable, 2026-06-15) — `outputs/worked-event-model-customer-support-desk.md` · *standing-gap artifact, the sibling to yesterday's coding-factory pick*

   - **Why it's worth reading:** Yesterday's digest pointed you at the coding-factory model; this is its companion, and together they triangulate `[[event-modeled-agent-design]]` from both of the KB's flagship agent use cases (coding + customer support).
