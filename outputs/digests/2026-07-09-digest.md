**1. Simon Willison — "Understand to participate" (simonwillison.net, 2026-07-02)**
_TLDR:_ A short field note relaying Geoffrey Litt's AI Engineer talk. **2. Martin Dilger — "How I use Local-LLMs to build software systems" (Parts 1 & 2) (LinkedIn, 2026-07-02)**
_TLDR:_ The most concrete implementation report yet of Dilger's always-on modeling/build factory. Three on-prem Asus GX10 boxes run 6–10 agents in ralph-loops 24/7 (Gemma4 ~60 tok/s, Qwen3.6:27B ~30 tok/s; Claude Code / OpenCode over Ollama). Each project has a Build-Kit + Modeling-Kit wired live to the Eventmodelers board. Workflow: model with stakeholders → slice set to "Planned" → **one agent claims it and flips it to "In Progress" (the whole locking/synchronization trick)** → no collisions, no merge conflicts. Slices are the unit of parallelism; agents wake only when there's work ("no cost, no tokens"). Move a slice back to "Planned" and an agent diffs spec-vs-code and self-corrects. He calls EM + ES + slice-based architecture "the triplet of flexible architectures."
_Why worth reading:_ This is your tight EM×agents focus made real — hardware, models, and the claim-lock mechanism behind the "always-on agent" concept. It's the operational backing for `[[dilger-event-modeling-agent-harness]]` and `[[dilger-model-is-a-living-spec-always-on-agent]]`.
_Path:_ `raw/notes/dilger-local-llm-distributed-agent-setup-event-modeling.md` — filed, not yet ingested.

**4. Martin Dilger — "My Wife Asked Me to Build a Registration Form for a Flea Market" (LinkedIn, 2026-07-01)**
_TLDR:_ A story-shaped argument for model-first-even-when-trivial. Dilger models a flea-market registration with his non-technical wife by just drawing the screens (she follows along without ever hearing "Event Modeling"); modeled → built → deployed in under 30 minutes. Takeaways: the method is invisible plumbing, the user only wants the problem solved; the planning step "earns its keep" as complexity grows; and he'd use event sourcing even here because with the tooling pre-decided it's *simpler* than the alternatives — "one button click away from building."
_Why worth reading:_ A clean, quotable demonstration of the "no setup ceremony, model→code→deploy" payoff that the factory posts (item 2) describe at scale — useful evidence for the accessibility-of-EM thread and the vibe-modeling concept.
