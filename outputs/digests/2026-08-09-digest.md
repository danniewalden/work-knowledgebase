## Read next

**1. Fritzsche — "Thinking in Events" — *filed, not yet ingested* (freshest unprocessed input)**
`raw/articles/fritzsche-thinking-in-events.md` (ricofritzsche.me, published 2026-07-03, retrieved 2026-08-07)

- **TLDR:** Fritzsche's cleanest statement of a distinction the KB has only held in fragments: **Event Modeling is a modeling discipline; Event Sourcing is an implementation choice — and thinking in events does not require an event store.** Using a booking domain, he argues systems fail when business situations get compressed into technical structure (records, flags, status columns) too early, and that generic `ReservationUpdated`-style language "preserves the mutation and loses the reason." Event Modeling keeps the domain language (guest, host, no-show, cancellation, refund) and the *why* visible; the storage decision (relational tables vs. an event store) should then follow from real needs (auditability, temporal reasoning, independent read models) — not be smuggled in by the modeling method. Quotes Dymitruk's clean boundary: *"Events happen — whether we store them or not is our choice."*
- **Why it's worth reading:** It's the sharpest primary in the KB separating **EM the method from ES the store** — directly relevant to your focus substrate and to the recurring "does adopting DCB/ES change Event Modeling?" question (pairs with [[fritzsche-choosing-storage-is-choosing-what-your-system-forgets]] and [[dilger-how-does-dcb-affect-event-modeling]]). It's also the only source waiting to be turned into a `wiki/` page.
