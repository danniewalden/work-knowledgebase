**1. Software Factories, Light and Dark — Addy Osmani (addyosmani.com, Jul-20)**
`raw/articles/addyosmani-software-factories-light-and-dark.md` — *filed 07-23, not yet ingested*
TLDR: Osmani's fullest statement of the **loop → harness → factory** stack. A *loop* is one agent doing a single job on repeat (gather → act → check → repeat); a *harness* is the walls around it (sandbox, tools, surviving memory, done-gates); a *factory* is "an org chart made of loops" — many harnessed loops fed by a work queue and drained through one review gate. He splits factories into **dark** (code ships that no human has read — the FANUC/Xiaomi lights-out analogy, "in software, the floor is the diff") and **lit** (a human still reads what ships, and judgment moves *upstream* to product/design/architecture). The governing rule is **back pressure**: "hand a loop only as much autonomy as you can cheaply and reliably verify, and not one inch more" — verification, not generation, is the constraint (the wide-mouth/narrow-neck funnel). A loop earns the dark only with a cheap, high-frequency, un-fakeable green/red oracle over short 3–10-step runs; keep the lights on where a wrong call is expensive. Leans heavily on Dex Horthy/HumanLayer's AIEWF talk "Harness Engineering Is Not Enough," names **comprehension debt** ("the tests green the whole way"), and — answering the graph-vs-loop thread — argues owning your control flow is "walking the graph back around the loop" (LangGraph/LlamaIndex/state machines).
Why it's worth reading: this is the current apex of the loop-engineering thread you track. It adds the *factory* rung on top of [[loop-engineering]], hands you the light/dark + back-pressure vocabulary for deciding *what* to automate, and ties graphs-vs-loops directly into [[nick-tune-graphs-memory-skills-agents]]. Highest-leverage of the three.

**2. Earning taste and judgment — Addy Osmani (addyosmani.com, Jul-14)**
`raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment.md` — *filed 07-21, not yet ingested*
TLDR: The companion piece on human capital. Taste used to be a byproduct of "the reps"; agents took the reps, so juniors must now acquire judgment *on purpose*. Osmani marshals 2026 labour-market data (recent-grad unemployment 5.6% / underemployment 41.5%; AI-exposed 22–25 employment down ~16%; the Microsoft "sleep() to hide a race condition" cautionary tale) to argue the entry-level rung is thinning while judgment-heavy senior roles grow — the aggregate-growth and vanishing-first-rung stories are both true at once. He restates the inner-loop (agent: investigate/implement/test/report) vs outer-loop (human: worth attention? verify by evidence → approve/block → carry the consequence) boundary, names the **two debts** (cognitive surrender + comprehension), and gives seven concrete habits: read more code than you generate, keep a "wrong log," do some things the hard way, go deep on one system end-to-end, **specify and verify separately** ("specification quality is the biggest lever"), **build an eval/rubric and run it on 50 real AI PRs**, and calibrate autonomy per task.
Why it's worth reading: the "stay the engineer" side of the same argument — the eval-as-personal-calibration and specify-vs-verify habits are directly actionable, and it reinforces the accountability seam in [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]], [[willison-understand-to-participate]], and [[loop-engineering]].

**3. A Fireside Chat with Cat and Thariq from the Claude Code team — Simon Willison (simonwillison.net, Jul-21)**
`raw/articles/willison-fireside-chat-claude-code-team.md` — *filed 07-25, not yet ingested (freshest capture in the KB)*
TLDR: Willison's edited-and-annotated transcript of his AIEWF fireside with two Anthropic Claude Code PMs (Cat Wu, Thariq Shihipar). It's evidence-rich vendor practice rather than a thesis: **Claude Tag (Slack) now lands ~65% of the team's product-eng PRs**; humans are being removed from review for "outer-layer" changes over 6+ months of trust-building, with incident PRs added to an **eval set** so review quality never regresses; the Claude Code **system prompt shrank ~80%** because frontier models (Fable 5, Opus 4.8) do *worse* with examples and "don't do X" lists — "soften the prompt," give context not constraints, one system prompt per model; auto mode runs a Sonnet classifier over every tool call to gate the sandbox; and **Claude Tag memory = "a markdown file per channel."**
Why it's worth reading: a rare look at how a top team actually runs coding agents at scale — the maker≠checker/eval-set discipline, the prompting inversion, and the on-disk-memory primitive all corroborate your harness/loop-engineering pages from the vendor side. Caveat: it's an interview transcript, so lighter on argument than Osmani's essays.
