## Read next

**1. Oskar Dudycz — "Vertical slices, their ownership and external dependencies"** (Event-Driven.io, 2026-08-10)
`raw/articles/dudycz-vertical-slices-ownership-and-external-dependencies.md` — *filed, not yet ingested*

*TLDR:* Dudycz answers the question the KB's [[vertical-slice-architecture]] page leaves open — what a slice does when it needs something it doesn't own. His answer: treat **everything outside the slice as one category of "external"** (the next folder, the parent module, a third-party API, the database), have the slice **declare a narrow function type in its own vocabulary** (`CheckContractor = (id) => Promise<'Active'|'Unknown'|'Suspended'>`) instead of importing a wide provider interface, and compose by hand-rolled partial application in one file near the entry point — no container, no registration. He also untangles the vocabulary: a **slice is one piece of functionality cut through the app, "more a function than an entity"**; a **module is a grouping of slices** (criterion: what changes together); a **bounded context is a linguistic barrier, not a folder** — "seven features of one application are almost always slices, or at most modules, sitting inside a single context", and frontend/backend are deployment targets, not contexts. Consequences: module-level cycles dissolve because neither module imports the other; persistence splits three ways (logic per entity, read model per query, **schema per module** — "a schema per slice gives you migrations that correspond to nothing"); and his closing section makes the locality argument an **agent** argument — "a layered structure that needs five folders touched for one feature costs an agent the same way it costs us, faster."

*Why it's worth reading:* It's the substrate piece with the best claim on your business-capabilities thread — his "group by what changes together / what shares a vocabulary" criterion is capability-shaped boundary-drawing without the DDD baggage — and its closing paragraph is independent, non-vendor, non-.NET corroboration of [[miller-codebase-is-the-prompt-vertical-slices-ai]] and [[locality-of-reference]].

**2. Laurie Voss — "What the Hell Is a Loop, Anyway?"** (O'Reilly Radar, 2026-07-29; orig. LinkedIn)
`raw/articles/voss-what-the-hell-is-a-loop-anyway.md` — *filed, not yet ingested (out-of-window backfill, needs your call)*

*TLDR:* Voss's claim is that "loop" now names **at least four distinct architectures**, and he gives each an iteration unit, an exit condition and a human role. (1) **Execution loop** — the agent's own act→observe→decide cycle; ends on environment feedback, "and also whenever the agent decides it's done, whether or not it actually is." (2) **Task loop** — Huntley's Ralph loop; restart against the same spec with a fresh context window every iteration, one task per loop, because "the apparent waste is the point" — refeeding the spec prevents context rot; the human is a "locomotive engineer" watching for failure patterns. (3) **Product loop** — the software factory; Warp's Zach Lloyd enumerates triage→spec→implementation→review→verification→shipping→monitoring and an adoption path that ratchets auto-merge from ~20% toward 60%; closing signals come from *outside* the codebase. (4) **System loop / "autoresearch"** (Gavrilescu) — the outer loop studies and maintains the primary system, iterating on prompts, harnesses, models and the evals themselves; "the loop is the product", with Karpathy's ~630-line overnight autoresearch and Meta's Brain2Qwerty v2 as existence proofs — both still hand-picking the final config. Two bonuses: he **names the "???? loop"** at the top of swyx's diagram the **oversight loop** ("set goals, allocate, cull" — the one ring where a human should live), and he gives a negative definition that tightens everything: Cognition's Agentic MapReduce is *not* a loop, because "a loop without feedback is just a for statement."

*Why it's worth reading:* Your [[loop-engineering]] page currently collapses all four of these into one, and this map lets every captured primary be re-filed against *which* loop it's actually about — plus it's the cleanest account the KB would hold of the AIEWF closing debate (Horthy's "deterministic control loops, step down an abstraction level" vs Litt's "those who delegate understanding get replaced", with Krieger conceding Anthropic's Tag team is bottlenecked on human review).

**3. Kief Morris — "Humans and Agents in Software Engineering Loops"** (martinfowler.com, "Exploring Gen AI", 2026-03-04)
`raw/articles/morris-humans-and-agents-in-software-engineering-loops.md` — *filed, not yet ingested (out-of-window backfill, needs your call)*

*TLDR:* Morris splits the **"why loop"** (idea → working software; humans run it "because we're the ones who want what it produces") from the **"how loop"** (the intermediate artefacts — code, tests, tooling, ADRs — which "are really just a means to an end"), notes the how loop is itself nested (feature → story → code), then names the three human positions: **outside the loop** (vibe coding, and "some interpretations of Spec Driven Development"), **in the loop** (per-line gatekeeper in the innermost loop → "we become a bottleneck… humans spending more time specifying and reviewing code than they save"), and **on the loop** — build and manage the harness. His one-line test is the sharpest in the literature: *"The 'in the loop' way is to fix the artefact. The 'on the loop' way is to change the harness that produced the artefact so it produces the results we want."* He also names **"the agentic flywheel"** (a harness that generates recommendations for improving itself, fed by tests/evals, pipeline stages, production data and commercial results, with a maturity path from interactive review → backlog items → auto-approval above a risk score), and closes honestly: at the limit that "might look a lot like humans out of the loop, old-school vibe coding."

*Why it's worth reading:* It predates by four months — and independently anticipates — [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]] (Jul-15) and LangChain's hill-climbing loop, both of which the KB already holds, so [[loop-engineering]] and [[harness-engineering]] currently under-credit their own venue. It also supplies the internal-quality argument [[ai-readable-code]] is missing ("when LLMs can more quickly understand and modify the code they work faster and spiral less").
