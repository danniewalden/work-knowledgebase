**1. Martin Dilger — The Event Modeling Agent Harness** · `[[dilger-event-modeling-agent-harness]]` · *new wiki page (focus)*
**TLDR:** Dilger names and diagrams a concrete harness where agents run 24/7 in a ralph-loop "looking for work," and the **Event Modeling slice is the unit of work**, advanced through a Draft→Ready→In Progress→Done board. The model (especially its Given-When-Then scenarios) is the BDD feedback backbone; cheap local models (Qwen3.6 on Ollama) do the coding once a "blue-print architecture" makes it "painting by the numbers," with Sonnet reserved for modeling/review. Punchline: if a task needs huge reasoning or a million-token window, that's usually a sign of **coupling, not a model limit**.
**Why it's worth reading:** This is the most explicit operating statement yet of the EM×agents thesis at the center of your focus — it fuses the EM slice with harness machinery and lands on the same "keep it local and loosely coupled" conclusion as Miller/Fritzsche/Khononov.

**5. AI-Readable Code** · `[[ai-readable-code]]` · *new concept hub (focus)*
**TLDR:** A synthesis hub for the "design the codebase for the agent, not just the human" strand: code should be optimized so an agent can read, reason about, and safely modify it, because change cost is dominated by reconstruction work and blast radius. Converges five voices — Tornhill's **CLEAR**, Khononov's **Balanced Coupling**, Miller's "codebase is the prompt," Fritzsche's functional-core/imperative-shell, and OpenAI's legibility rule — onto one claim: make intent and boundaries explicit and local.
**Why it's worth reading:** It's the connective tissue for the whole design-substrate thread you broadened into; reading it once saves drilling into five separate source pages, and its open question (a definition mapping CLEAR↔coupling↔locality↔legibility) is a natural next deliverable.

**TLDR:** Coined on podcast Ep 19 as Event Modeling's answer to "vibe coding": rather than loosely prompting an LLM and accepting the output, sketch the system as an event model *with* stakeholders and AI first, so the "vibes" become an explicit information-flow blueprint. **Why it's worth reading:** Short and stub-state, but it's the podcast's hands-on framing of the model-as-guardrail idea at the heart of event-modeled-agent-design — worth a glance to see whether it merits growing into a full page as more episodes are mined.
