2. **Osmani — "Software Factories, Light and Dark"** (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-20) — *filed 07-23, not yet ingested; highest-value backlog item*
   `raw/articles/addyosmani-software-factories-light-and-dark.md`
   **TLDR:** Osmani's first dedicated primary on the "software factory" — *harnessing loops at scale*: "the loop is the atom; the factory is the loop at scale… an org chart made of loops, not a bigger agent." Core distinction is **light vs dark factory**: a *dark* factory ships code no human has read (verified only by machine oracles — the lights-out FANUC/Xiaomi analogy, "in software the floor is the diff"), a *lit* factory keeps humans where judgment lives and moves review **upstream** to product/design/architecture. Argues "harness engineering is not enough" (leaning on Dex Horthy/HumanLayer's AIEWF talk), restates **back-pressure** ("hand a loop as much autonomy as you can cheaply and reliably verify, not one inch more" — verification, not generation, is the constraint), and closes the **loops-vs-graphs-vs-state-machines** question ("owning your control flow is really just walking the graph back around the loop") that the 07-20 run had flagged.
   **Why it's worth reading:** It adds the **factory rung** on top of your loop-engineering thread and directly answers the graph-engineering framing tied to Nick Tune's Graph+Memory+Skills substrate — the clearest map yet of where humans stay in an agent-scaled org, which is squarely your EM×agents governance concern.

3. **Osmani — "Earning taste and judgment"** (addyosmani.com, 2026-07-14) — *filed 07-21, not yet ingested*
   `raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment.md`
   **TLDR:** "Taste used to be a byproduct of the reps. Agents took the reps." Osmani argues taste and judgment came from thousands of boilerplate/bug-fix reps, not talent — so once agents automate those reps, juniors must acquire judgment *deliberately*. Restates the inner-loop (agent: investigate/implement/test/report) vs outer-loop (human: worth-attention? verify by evidence — diffs/tests/logs/why — approve or block) boundary; names "two debts: cognitive surrender + comprehension"; gives 7 concrete habits, including **build a rubric/eval (correctness, maintainability, efficiency, security, style) and run it on 50 real AI PRs** as personal calibration, and "specification quality is the biggest lever — specify and verify separately."
   **Why it's worth reading:** The human-capital companion to the factory piece; the "build an eval on 50 real PRs" habit is a concrete grader/verification-loop practice, and the spec-quality-as-lever point ties straight to your spec-driven / Event-Model-as-spec seam.
