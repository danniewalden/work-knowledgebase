## Read next

### 1. Birgitta Böckeler — "Understanding Spec-Driven-Development: Kiro, spec-kit, and Tessl"
*martinfowler.com "Exploring Gen AI", 2025-10-15 · `raw/articles/bockeler-understanding-sdd-kiro-speckit-tessl.md` · **filed, not yet ingested***

**TLDR.** Böckeler tries three SDD tools and untangles the term into the three-rung taxonomy the KB already borrows as working vocabulary — **spec-first** (write a spec, use it for this task), **spec-anchored** (keep it for maintenance), **spec-as-source** (only the spec is edited; the human never touches code). Her definition of a spec is deliberately narrow: a structured, behavior-oriented natural-language artifact, distinct from the general "memory bank" context of rules and codebase descriptions. The section nothing else in the KB covers is the **MDD parallel**: she built code generators over UML/textual-DSL models early in her career, and argues LLMs remove MDD's overhead (no parseable spec language, no generator to build) at the price of non-determinism — *"I wonder if spec-as-source, and even spec-anchoring, might end up with the downsides of both MDD and LLMs: Inflexibility and non-determinism."* Her closing worry is a German compound: **Verschlimmbesserung** — making something worse in the attempt to make it better.

**Why it's worth reading.** This is the strongest available *prior* for Dilger's model-as-language position and the sharpest available warning against it, from one source — MDD's parseable structure is precisely what bought tool support for writing valid, complete, consistent specs, which is Dilger's entire argument for a schema'd Event Model, and MDD still didn't take off. It lands directly on [[model-as-code-vs-model-as-language]], the KB's one live unresolved controversy.

### 2. Dan McAteer — "The Evolution of the Agent Harness"
*Latent.Space, 2026-08-22 · `raw/articles/mcateer-evolution-of-the-agent-harness.md` · **filed, not yet ingested** (Batch F spine)*

**TLDR.** McAteer's answer to "what changed around Christmas 2025" is that the model curve and the harness curve crossed, not that either alone jumped — he quotes Lukasz Kaiser conceding it is "not that easy to pin down what did it." From there he argues the **absorption thesis**: models keep absorbing harness functionality into their weights, engineers keep deleting what got absorbed, and *"what remains is a harness for **human attention** rather than for the model."* He carries the quantitative anchor this whole thread lacks — the same model over the same **106 tasks** scores **52.4 to 76.2 across different harnesses**, a 23.8-point spread with zero change to the model. "Half the agent is the harness."

**Why it's worth reading.** *Harness absorption* has **zero presence anywhere in the wiki**, and [[agent-harness]] at ~61 lines demonstrably cannot carry it. This is also the one number that would move [[loop-engineering]] off being built almost entirely from advocate self-report — and per `outputs/batch-f-plan-2026-08-31.md` it's file 3 of the 4-file definitional spine that should be ingested before anything else in the loop cluster.

### 3. Oskar Dudycz — "Vertical slices, their ownership and external dependencies"
*Event-Driven.io, 2026-08-10 · `raw/articles/dudycz-vertical-slices-ownership-and-external-dependencies.md` · **filed, not yet ingested — 22 days waiting, the oldest on-focus capture in the queue***

**TLDR.** Dudycz takes five recurring vertical-slice questions ("do we get a table per slice?", "how can a *verify order* slice check what a *register order* slice wrote if they aren't supposed to know about each other?") and shows they are all one question: **what does a slice do when it needs something from outside?** The damage, he argues, comes from calling every area of an application a bounded context — that sets the separation bar as high as it goes, and then any connection looks like a violation. His disentangling: a **slice** is one piece of functionality cut through the whole app, more a *function* than an entity ("verify a transport order"); a **module** is a grouping of slices by what changes together; a **bounded context** is a *linguistic* barrier, not a deployment one — frontend and backend are two deployment targets, not two contexts. Seven features of one app are almost always slices, or at most modules, inside a single context. And: *minimise isn't zero*.

**Why it's worth reading.** The wiki wrote **[[slice]]** yesterday to resolve exactly this conflation, and built it from Dilger and Bogard while the sharpest third treatment sat un-ingested in `raw/`. Dudycz adds the middle rung the new page doesn't have — *module* — and the explicit VSA↔slice ownership mapping the watch config has wanted since June.

### 4. Colin Eberhardt (CTO, Scott Logic) — "Putting Spec Kit Through Its Paces: Radical Idea or Reinvented Waterfall?"
*Scott Logic Blog, 2025-11-26 · `raw/articles/eberhardt-putting-spec-kit-through-its-paces.md` · **filed, not yet ingested***

**TLDR.** The "~10x slower" trial the KB has been citing secondhand since 2026-08-16, with the actual numbers: rebuilding one feature of a **hobby app**, Spec Kit produced **2,577 lines of markdown, 689 loc, 33m30 of agent time and 3.5 hours of review**, against **1,000 loc, 8 minutes of agent time and 24 minutes of review** for his ordinary iterative approach — and the SDD run shipped a bug the iterative one didn't. His verdict is measured rather than hostile: "an interesting thought experiment, I'm not sure it is a practical approach." The argument the KB lacked is his: *"Code is law because it is a formal language you can reason about… specifications in markdown lack this formality."*

**Why it's worth reading.** That last line is [[jeremy-miller]]'s position stated eight months earlier by someone with no Critter Stack to sell, and it answers Dilger's "we have a formal language — it's called Code" from the *opposite* conclusion. It is also the load-bearing evidence under [[spec-driven-development]]'s counter-case, which has rested on paraphrase for two weeks.

### 5. **[[slice]]** — new concept page

**Why it's worth reading.** Its headline finding is the one that matters for everything else you're tracking: **every agent-facing claim in this knowledgebase rests on the model unit, not the code unit** — and until yesterday that distinction existed nowhere. ### 6. Gojko Adzic — "Spec Driven Development – revenge of Waterfall or BDD taken to new level?"
*LinkedIn Pulse, 2025-09-29 · `raw/articles/adzic-spec-driven-development-revenge-of-waterfall-or-bdd.md` · **filed, not yet ingested — short***

**TLDR.** The earliest of the four SDD critiques (three weeks after Spec Kit launched) and the warmest: Adzic finds the enforced constitution→specification→plan→tasks flow "mimics a lot of what I am currently doing when using Claude Code, and makes it more systematic," and rates exposing the tool's conclusions as editable, version-controlled files a genuinely good way to keep a human in the loop. His real objections are elsewhere and sharper than the one he was quoted for: generated specs are **scope-of-work, not specification** ("this is not a spec, it lacks a ton of detail"), the actual spec migrates into developer-readable tests — *"a missed opportunity"* from the Specification-by-Example tradition — and Spec Kit has no **scoping phase**, so it "went off the rails" until human-in-the-loop stopped being feasible.

**Why it's worth reading.** Short, and it's the correction in your own KB's rear-view mirror — the wiki amplified his headline as an "even the BDD pioneer says so" move for a fortnight before the capture disproved it. His missing-scoping-phase complaint also converges independently with Dilger's four-phase argument, which is a stronger pairing than either alone.
