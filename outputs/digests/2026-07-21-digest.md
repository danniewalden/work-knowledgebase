## Read next

**1. Addy Osmani — "Earning taste and judgment"** (AddyOsmani.com, Jul 14 2026) · `raw/articles/addyosmani-earning-taste-and-judgment.md` · *filed 07-21, not yet ingested*

TLDR: Osmani's argument is that taste and judgment used to be a *byproduct of doing the reps* (boilerplate, bug fixes, code review) — and now agents have taken the reps, so juniors must acquire judgment deliberately instead of by osmosis. He backs the "vanishing first rung" with hard 2026 labor data (recent-grad unemployment 5.6% / underemployment 41.5%; junior tech postings down 34% since 2020; Stanford's 16% employment decline for 22–25-year-olds) while being honest that it's partly a post-2022 hiring-binge correction, not purely AI. The prescription is the durable, *ungradeable* human part: own the outer loop (worth-my-attention? verify by evidence → approve/block → carry the consequence), and build taste via seven concrete habits — read far more code than you generate, keep a "wrong log", do things the hard way on purpose, specify and verify separately, and build a personal eval/rubric run on 50 real AI PRs.

Why it's worth reading: it's the keystone that completes Osmani's loop-engineering arc alongside [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]] and [[willison-understand-to-participate]] — and its "build an eval, calibrate autonomy, specification is the biggest lever" habits map straight onto your verification-loop / spec-driven-development / back-pressure threads. This is the single freshest unprocessed input in the KB right now.

**2. "Agentic Harness Engineering: Observability-Driven Automatic Evolution of Coding-Agent Harnesses"** (arXiv 2604.25850, Lin/Liu/Dou/Gui et al., Fudan/Peking, Apr 2026) · *referenced in the watch log, NOT yet in `raw/` — capture candidate*

TLDR: A closed-loop system in which an agent *autonomously evolves its own harness* via three observability pillars (component, experience, decision), with every harness edit framed as a falsifiable prediction. Ten iterations lift Terminal-Bench-2 pass@1 from 69.7→77.0% (beating human-designed Codex-CLI's 71.9% and ACE/TF-GRPO baselines) and transfer to SWE-bench-verified at 75.6% with −12% tokens.

Why it's worth reading: it's the peer-review-grade empirical realization of the **hill-climbing / self-improving loop** — LangChain's fourth stacked loop ([[langchain-the-art-of-loop-engineering]]) — a rung the loop-engineering thread currently supports only with practitioner essays. It's been flagged "recommend ingesting next" across the 07-13, 07-20 and 07-21 runs; worth deciding on.
