### 1. Loop Engineering — new concept page *(new wiki page; not yet in index)*
`[[loop-engineering]]` — created 06-29 from Osmani + LangChain + swyx

**TLDR.** The named layer *one floor above* [[harness-engineering]]: stop being the person who prompts the agent, design the control system that prompts, verifies, and stops it. Coined June 2026 (swyx's "Loopcraft"; Steinberger/Cherny "I write loops, not prompts"; Karpathy "remove yourself as the bottleneck"). Two complementary models: LangChain's **four stacked loops** (agent → verification/grader → event-driven/always-on → hill-climbing, where an analysis agent reads traces and rewrites the harness config) and Osmani's **five primitives + memory** (automations, worktrees, skills, MCP connectors, sub-agents, on-disk memory). Carries three sharpening caveats — verification is still yours, comprehension debt, "cognitive surrender."

**Why it's worth reading.** This is the biggest new synthesis in the KB this week and a direct evolution of your [[ralph-loop]] / [[long-running-agents]] / [[unattended-coding-agents]] thread — and its open seam is squarely on your focus: *could an Event Model supply the loop's goals and stop conditions* (slice = unit of work, GWT = the verification rubric), tying it to [[dilger-event-modeling-agent-harness]].

### 2. Fritzsche — "The real concern is the domain capability, not horizontal layers" *(new wiki page; not yet in index)*
`[[fritzsche-clean-architecture-capability-over-layers]]` — source page from `raw/notes/fritzsche-clean-architecture-untouchable-capability-over-layers` (LinkedIn, 2026-06-28)

**TLDR.** A pointed critique of the Clean Architecture diagram: Dependency Inversion only swaps a concrete dependency for an abstract one — the business logic still depends on I/O, and the functional dependencies inside the behavior don't go away. Separation of Concerns isn't achieved by horizontal technical layers; *the real concern is the domain capability*. CRUD is not domain language (experts think in processes, decisions, state transitions), and Functional Core / Imperative Shell is the stronger foundation for coherent capabilities. Credits Ralf Westphal's work on functional dependencies.

**Why it's worth reading.** Directly advances **business-capabilities** (your flagged-important area): it sharpens the capability-as-the-boundary argument against the most widely-shared layering diagram, and reinforces the through-line in [[autonomous-domain-capabilities]] and [[rico-fritzsche-rpu-reactor-vocabulary]].

### 3. Dilger — "The harness and the code are just 20% — the first 80% is the requirements" *(new wiki page; not yet in index)*
`[[dilger-harness-is-20-percent-requirements-are-80]]` — source page from `raw/notes/` (LinkedIn, 2026-06-29)

**TLDR.** Dilger's sharpest framing yet of his recurring thesis: everyone is building AI harnesses (agents, roles, skills, guardrails), but the harness + code are only **20%** of the solution. The other **80%** is clarifying requirements and getting business processes into a format that works — "nobody tells you how to write that damn markdown." It's a human/communications problem with *no technical solution*; he focuses on that first 80% (via Event Modeling), after which "the rest becomes pretty easy."

**Why it's worth reading.** Tight EM×agents and a clean counter-weight to the loop-engineering enthusiasm in item 1 — it argues the leverage is *upstream* of the harness, tying [[harness-engineering]] ↔ [[event-modeled-agent-design]] ↔ [[spec-driven-development]].

### 4. Fritzsche — "An atomic append isn't enough; CCC needs a protected write order" *(new wiki page; not yet in index)*
`[[fritzsche-ccc-atomic-append-serialized-write-order]]` — source page from `raw/notes/` (LinkedIn, 2026-06-23)

**TLDR.** The implementation layer under Command Context Consistency. A PostgreSQL CTE that combines the Event Query check with the insert makes each append *atomic* — but under READ COMMITTED two concurrent commands can observe the same context version, reach identical decisions, and *both* append. CCC needs a **protected write order** (serialization), e.g. locking a single metadata row per append txn: physical writes serialized globally, the conflict decision stays local to the command context. "Atomicity safeguards one append; serialization prevents two decisions from the same observed context being accepted." The lock is an implementation detail — the contract is what matters.

**Why it's worth reading.** The most concrete substrate post in a while — it turns the [[dynamic-consistency-boundaries]] / [[autonomous-domain-capabilities]] discussion from principle into a buildable invariant (and a caveat for anyone implementing the "append-rejection" rule yourself).

### 5. Fritzsche — "Microservices are not a maturity level" *(new wiki page; not yet in index)*
`[[fritzsche-microservices-not-a-maturity-level]]` — source page from `raw/notes/` (LinkedIn, 2026-06-24)

**TLDR.** Pushes back on the "learn the modular monolith first, then earn microservices" framing (naming Anton Martyniuk). Microservices aren't a reward or a maturity tier — they're an architectural decision with a concrete cost and purpose, justified only by a real reason: independent deployment, different scaling, strong business boundaries, or **organizational ownership** (a team that truly owns a service end to end). Without that ownership you get organizational mess, not autonomy. The real questions: what's the goal, what are the boundaries, who owns what, and what structure can carry that responsibility.

**Why it's worth reading.** A boundaries/ownership lens on **business-capabilities** — same "capability + ownership is the unit" instinct as items 2 and 4, applied to the monolith-vs-microservices debate. Lighter than the others; skim if short on time.

### 6. Kulkarni / Thoughtworks — "Building Reliable Agentic AI Systems" (Bayer PRINCE) *(raw, still un-ingested — waiting since 06-25)*
`raw/articles/martinfowler-prince-building-reliable-agentic-ai-systems.md`

**TLDR.** A rare *independent, real-world* production case study (published on martinfowler.com): Bayer's PRINCE preclinical research assistant (agentic RAG + Text-to-SQL), framed explicitly through **context engineering** and **harness engineering**. LangGraph multi-agent orchestration with three reflection loops (process / data / draft); reliability via LLM fallbacks, node-level retries, and PostgreSQL state-persistence recovery; Langfuse/RAGAS evals on curated datasets *and* daily live traffic; per-sentence citations + human-in-the-loop for trust. Core lesson: "production-ready agentic AI is not about better models or prompts — it's engineering both the context the model sees and the harness within which it acts."

**Why it's worth reading.** The strongest non-vendor corroboration the harness/context-engineering thread has, and it now reads as a real-world instance of the loop-engineering pattern (item 1) — a regulated enterprise independently arriving at the KB's vocabulary.
