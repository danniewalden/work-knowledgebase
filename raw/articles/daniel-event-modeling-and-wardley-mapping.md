## Thesis (summarized, second-hand)

Wardley Mapping and Event Modeling are presented as **complementary** practices: **Wardley Mapping**
positions the organization's components/capabilities on a value-chain (visibility) × evolution
(genesis→commodity) plane to reason about **strategy** — where to invest, build vs. buy, what is
differentiating vs. utility. **Event Modeling** then turns the chosen direction into a **concrete,
deterministic system design** (the timeline of commands/events/read-models). In short: Wardley says
*where to go and why*; Event Modeling says *what to build*. A 4-part series walks through using them
together.

## Why it matters

This is the documented bridge between [[business-capabilities]] / strategy and [[event-modeling]]:
capabilities (Homann) tell you the stable "what"; Wardley maps tell you how each capability is
*evolving* and therefore how to treat it strategically; Event Modeling renders the resulting design.
Relevant to Dannie's flagged-important business-capabilities focus. (Adam Dymitruk has also referenced
an EM × Wardley connection in his talks.)
