# Spec-Driven Development applied

**Martin Dilger** — *LinkedIn feed post, 2d ago (edited)*

---

Spec-Driven Development applied.

Prompt. Hope. Pray. Argue. Throw hands in the air. Do it yourself. I bet you know that cycle.

My early attempts with AI agents were miserable. I was spending more time wrestling with the AI than building anything.
The industry's early answer was "prompt engineering" - a desperate attempt to force a language model to behave through better wording. I tried it. Everyone did. It didn't work.

AI is like a teenager. It doesn't care what you say. When you get angry, you get a "you are absolutely right" that means absolutely nothing.
The more I tried to control it, the worse it got.

The fix wasn't a better prompt. It was a better environment.

I already had Event Modeling in my toolkit - a process that forces clarity of intent before a single line of code gets written. I just hadn't connected the dots to AI yet.

Slowly I realized: every process I had that worked well without AI worked even better with AI. Not because I prompted better. Because those processes made it easier to do the right thing than the wrong thing.

You can't force an agent. But you can design an environment where good behavior is the path of least resistance - and build feedback loops that constantly answer one question: am I holding this right?

That's the whole game.
