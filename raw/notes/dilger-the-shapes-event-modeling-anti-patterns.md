There are 4 Pattern and 4 Anti-Pattern in Event Modeling.

Last year at the AxonIQ conference, people kept pulling me aside to review their event models. After the fifth or sixth one, I stopped zooming in. The shape alone told the story.

Because of that, I started calling them "The Shapes": the bed (one screen, many commands), the left chair (one command, many events), the right chair (one read model, many events), the shelf (one slice, all scenarios).

Only the bed is a hard red flag. The rest aren't wrong by default - they're warnings. Places to look closer before you commit to a design. Once you see them, you can't unsee them.

It´s an easy way to spot problems in your Models.

My most used AI-Skill in the Eventmodelers Plattform finds them and calls them out. Just use "/wdyt" and it´ll go through the model, ask meaningful questions, challenge assumptions and call out anti-patterns, where they make sense.

I use this skill all the time. Using AI in modeling, in building and as a sparring-partner.

Those questions are very valuable and often lead to "ah yes, didn´t think of this. Thanks!" - same you would say to a colleague walking past, who pointed something out.. "That looks weird there. Coffee?"
