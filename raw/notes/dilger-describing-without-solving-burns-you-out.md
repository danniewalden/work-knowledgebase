Did you feel it as well? Burned out, exhausted after each day, even though you "just" instructed some agents? somehow more productive on paper, but at what cost?

I've been there too. Early on, I ran 5 agent sessions in parallel, overseeing my agents like a kindergartner. And my brain was mud every evening.
A few years ago, we were obsessed with protecting people from context switching. Now we call the same thing productivity.

"I love writing code, I don't want agents to do that anymore." - Is that you?

Most probably you don´t love writing code. You love solving problems. So do I.

You cannot outtype an agent. That's like trying to outrun a car. It makes no sense, and yet you still try sometimes.

Actually writing the code is the most boring part. You maybe just don't know it yet, because for your whole career solving a problem always came together with typing it out. So your brain naturally connected the two.

This is also where spec-driven development goes wrong for a lot of teams.

They stop solving problems and just describe them, and then hand it to AI and hope it figures out the solution. That's not being in charge, that's checking out before it gets interesting.

And that´s what slowly burns you out. Describing a problem without solving it leaves a whole that keeps growing. I know, because I felt it too.

Describing your way through a day can feel just as hollow as mindlessly typing through one.

Done right, spec-driven development still means you're in charge of solving the problem. You hand over the boring part.

Next time that reluctance creeps in, ask yourself when you last felt the kick of actually solving something. If you can't remember, that's not AI's fault.
