Architects love to draw boxes and arrows - that's so 2024. In 2026 we use sticky notes and arrows 💪

Kidding aside - Diagrams are great for discussions, but oftentimes after a week, nobody knows what they mean anymore because sketching can go wild.

The same happens to Event Models.. they degrade over time if nobody owns them and takes care. Structure is king.

That´s why I´m a huge fan of "Planning like it´s Excel".

The Excel-idea initially felt off. The longer I thought about it though, the more it made sense.

Whiteboards give you total freedom. Draw anywhere, connect anything. Feels great in the room. In practice it goes wrong constantly - people move things, invent new patterns mid-session simply because the medium lets them, not because it's needed. Six months later, nobody outside the room can read the board.

The "Excel-structure" forces you to go from left to right. It enforces a plot, a storyboard. You can't jump around and lose the thread, because the grid won't let you.

It also gives every element a clear coordinate, like a cell reference. I can tell an AI "add a field in B3, adjust all dependents" and it means something precise. It talks back the same way - on a real project it once flagged: "there's a problem in B3, adding items might be missing the price."

I built eventmodelers.ai around the Excel idea. Humans and Agents love it alike..

Clear structure isn't nostalgia. It's what makes a spec legible to a human and a AI at once - and why both get it immediately.
