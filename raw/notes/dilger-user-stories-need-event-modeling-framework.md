# Dilger — User stories give you a sentence structure but no framework; Event Modeling supplies the missing timeline

*(Martin Dilger, LinkedIn, ~1d old at retrieval. Verbatim capture. Method-wide Event Modeling thread: a concrete client anecdote arguing that a bare user-story template is a sentence structure with no framework behind it — Event Modeling's visual timeline is the missing scaffold, and user stories can then be generated straight from the model. Extends [[dilger-harness-is-20-percent-requirements-are-80]], [[spec-driven-development]], [[event-modeling]].)*

Are you struggling writing user stories? You are not alone.

A potential client called me last week. IT had mandated all requirements delivered as user stories from now on. They got a template and a thumbs up. Nothing more.

A user story hands you a sentence structure and leaves you alone with it. There´s no framework behind it, no way to know what's missing. So they sat in front of an empty page. Paralyzed. Writers Block. What to write? How much context to give? Where to even start - high level, details?

Weeks went by like this. And nothing improved.

Then an engineer in the company, who had read my book, mentioned my name. "Talk to Martin".. That's how we ended up talking.

I showed them the method in 15 minutes. It clicked right away - because it solved the exact problem they'd been stuck on for weeks. What they were missing wasn't discipline. It was a visual way to design and model the business solution.

Add a timeline, and the writers block disappears - you always know what comes next.

Often it's just a shared language between business and engineering that unlocks everything. And the user stories they were told to write? Easy to generate straight from the timeline. They stop inventing them from nothing.

They haven´t booked yet, I hope they will. This will happen if they do.

A few weeks in, they will get a question from IT: what changed? Those stories have gotten a lot better.

A little later, they will realize, they don´t even need to translate to user stories. They can just hand over the modeled timeline itself. Engineers are much happier with that.

Then for small use cases, they will sit together to clarify some questions. Directly adjusting the model as they speak. And they will realize, they move 60-80% faster like that.

Finally they will start to do the timeline together right from the start. Removing the unnecessary handovers, no waiting, no guessing.
