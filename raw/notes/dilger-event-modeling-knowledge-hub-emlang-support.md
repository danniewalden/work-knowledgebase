(Verbatim LinkedIn post text; captured via live logged-in Chrome.)

This is my vision for Event Modeling - and for the future of our industry.

It's also the core reason I built the Event Modelers Platform and my main driver.

It's not that we don't know what our systems should be doing. It's that this knowledge is scattered everywhere - tickets, Confluence pages, code, Slack threads. A huge amount of it just lives in the heads of the people who've worked on the system for years.

Inaccessible, unless you already know where to look.
I'm on a mission to break that shell.

Event Modeling isn't "modeled software." It's a knowledge hub - one place to go when you want to understand how a system actually works. Simple language, simple terms, simple patterns to describe even complex problems.
But that's only half the vision.

Once the model is built and the knowledge is captured, it becomes the blueprint for implementation - for humans and for agents alike. Here we connect the dots.

Now we just need to start using it.

I don't care whether you model directly on the platform, write local YAML files and import them later, use JSON, use Markdown, or have an agent add knowledge via MCP. The format isn't the point. Access is.

That's also why I just added EmLang support to the platform - a YAML dialect for describing systems. I don't agree with every choice EmLang makes, but it's a solid starting point, and engineers especially love its simplicity. So I'm opening the platform up to it.

You can now import your EmLang models and make them accessible to anyone.
Breaking the shell, one model at a time.
