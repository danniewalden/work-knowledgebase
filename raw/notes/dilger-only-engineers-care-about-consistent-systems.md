Only engineers care about absolutely consistent systems.

We were building an app to optimize the experience for sales agents in physical stores. I was the technical Lead, and we were shaping the roadmap. We had all ideas sketched out, just needed some guidance on prioritization. So we thought..
I was about to learn the most important lesson of my career.

We had a tight deadline, were short on budget - so we decided to skip the usual chain and talked straight to the agents, the people working with real clients in stores every day.

Turns out - What we thought would be useful meant absolutely nothing to them.

We fell trap to the typical engineering thing. We tried to optimize everything, make the system consistent, blocking forms, enforcing the process.
It would have been a desaster.

Real people care about completely different things, and they're perfectly fine correcting things manually. Only engineers care about absolutely consistent systems.
Those few calls with real end-users probably shaped my career more than anything else.

I never forgot that.

It showed me how broken our typical workflows are: requirements engineers talking to product owners, product owners talking to business, nobody talking to real clients - everything reshaped a little at each hop before it reached us.

Now with AI - we are just adding one more hop to the chain.

Engineers talking to AI, writing tons of markdown.. one more handover.

I hate it. And I'm actively working on removing every single one of those handovers.

When was the last time you were talking to the end user instead of a Jira Ticket? Just give them a call, it might change everything.

Or even better - just invite them to a short Event Modeling Session. Show them what you planned, show them the screens you sketched. Ask the end user for feedback. Turns out - you do not have to write a single line of code for that.

( and using EM Studio, you can use the new Screen Preview to show hand-sketched screens, along Html-Mockups, along Figma-Screens in one Storyline )
