Two same-day LinkedIn posts by Adam Dymitruk, captured verbatim.

## Post 1 — "Only fundamentally sound and critical ideas will survive the AI disruption"
(https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7492280951054487552/)

Only fundamentally sound and critical ideas will survive the AI disruption. Current patterns and code artifacts are a black box. You have to go and try and use the software to see if it works. There is no standard way to see what's going on inside your solution. That gets incredibly time consuming and error prone as the solution grows. But with #EventModeling, you have a map for how everything works, giving you the control that no AI tooling gives you. In fact, what LLM you use doesn't matter that much. The way you see into the system is so much more important that we're at the point that choosing an LLM is like picking up pennies in front of a steamroller.

## Post 2 — "AI is melting the barrier to entry"
(https://www.linkedin.com/feed/update/urn:li:activity:7492295097636515840/)

AI is melting the barrier to entry for learning anything that isn't the default - no matter how dysfunctional and standardized that default is. We already saw it with the recent uptake in #Linux adoption over the last 2 years. People want the freedom, the customizability and moral high ground. Same goes for #EventSourcing. Why would you not want 100% accountability built into every system by default. Now leaning the right way from the start isn't going to cost you time and effort as before. I'm seeing these type of messages more and more. I've never been more optimistic about the future.
