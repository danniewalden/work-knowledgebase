# Agentwashing

[[gartner]]'s term (via [[svitla-agentic-ai-market-trends-2026]]) for marketing **assistants
as agents**. The underlying distinction:

- **AI assistant** — responds to prompts, depends on human direction at every step.
- **AI agent** — plans, uses tools, reasons over outcomes, and acts toward a goal with
  minimal oversight.

Most of what enterprises currently call "agents" are actually assistants. This is the cleanest
explanation for the **adoption-vs-production gap**: ~79% of enterprises say they've "adopted
AI agents" while only ~11% run them in production — because "adoption" can mean anything from
a support-page chatbot to an end-to-end autonomous resolver.

The gap is real because moving from a demo to production surfaces problems demos hide: data
access, security boundaries, error handling, compliance review, and integration with systems
never designed for autonomous agents. Maps directly onto [[agent-vs-workflow]] and the lower
rungs of the [[autonomy-ladder]].
