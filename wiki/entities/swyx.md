# swyx

Shawn "swyx" Wang — writer and AI-engineering commentator; founder of the **Latent.Space** podcast and
newsletter (which now includes the AINews daily roundup) and a principal popularizer of the term
"AI Engineer." In the KB he is the source of the **coinage** of [[loop-engineering]] via
[[swyx-loopcraft-art-of-stacking-loops|"Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops"]] (2026-06-12), where he
gathers the Steinberger / Boris Cherny / [[andrej-karpathy|Karpathy]] "loop discourse," frames the
strategic up/down asymmetry of stacking loops, and coins **"the Salty Lesson for agents"** (a riff on
Sutton's *Bitter Lesson*).

Functions here as a **synthesizer/curator** rather than a primary builder — his value is naming and
framing the pattern that [[addy-osmani|Osmani]] and [[langchain|LangChain]] then work out in mechanics.
He publishes at [[latent-space]], which he founded.

**A second capture, and a weak one (2026-09-03).** [[swyx-gpt6-astra-automated-ai-engineer]] covers the
GPT-6 Astra launch from an early-access seat and claims Astra-class models *"are fully capable AI Engineers
in their own right"* after *"20B+ tokens"* of self-chosen tasks. It is **an unfinished draft** (*"We are
out of time for this writeup"*), the author states the **access dependency himself** (*"OpenAI was most
generous with trial limits so this gets the writeup"* — **NOT INDEPENDENT**), and the capability claim is an
**IMPRESSION NOT MEASUREMENT** with no task set, baseline or failure accounting. The `<$6/hour` headline is
a token-rate calculation he contradicts two paragraphs later, and token volume is workload, not efficacy.
Its claims must not be allowed to stand on the strength of [[willison-gpt6-astra]], which handles the same
launch far more carefully.

**The pattern this page now records: a label arriving ahead of the evidence for it.** That is his
strength when the label is good (he coined [[loop-engineering]] before anyone had worked out the
mechanics) and its cost here. The one durable detail in the Astra piece is a described configuration:
fleets of subagents with individually tuned settings and **bounded concurrency**, with the agent starting
and stopping waves and monitoring its own runs — a **screenshot-backed description in an unfinished
draft**, so it should not be carried onto [[multi-agent-orchestration]] as practice.
