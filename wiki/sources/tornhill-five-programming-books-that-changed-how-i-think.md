# Tornhill — "Five Programming Books That Changed How I Think"

Source: [[adam-tornhill]], *Code for Humans and Machines* (Substack), 2026-08-11. Raw:
`raw/articles/tornhill-five-programming-books-that-changed-how-i-think.md`.

**Off-thread for this KB** — a personal reading list, not code-health or agentic material. Captured
because Tornhill is a tracked author (people watch = anything-substantive) and filed here mainly as a
reference page; two threads graze the KB's concerns and are noted below.

## Summary

A deliberately non-canonical top five, restricted to **pre-2015** books ("a decade is the time I need to
truly see the impact"), with the litmus test: did it change my coding style, and was the change
sustainable? On the classics he is brief — *Refactoring*, *DDD*, *Pragmatic Programmer*, *Working
Effectively with Legacy Code*, *Mythical Man-Month* all endorsed; **Clean Code explicitly not** ("too
narrow and a bit too dogmatic for my taste"), with Farley's *Modern Software Engineering* offered
instead.

His five (zero-indexed, so six): **Smalltalk Best Practice Patterns** (Beck, on coding style),
**SICP** (his all-time favourite; source of "wishful thinking" as a design tool — write code as if the
abstraction existed, then implement it), **Paradigms of AI Programming** (Norvig — the only book he has
seen demonstrate *comments as narrative explaining intent and reasoning*), **Facts and Fallacies of
Software Engineering** (Glass — "a book about an industry that refuses to learn"), **Thinking Forth**
(Brodie — programming as creative process; his model for technical writing), and **The Nature of Code**
(Shiffman — the book that kept him in software in 2013).

Honorable mentions: *The Art of the Metaobject Protocol*, *Let Over Lambda*, *The Design of Design*,
*The Little Schemer*, and **Multi-Paradigm Design for C++** (Coplien). Closing point: the lessons
transcend the Lisp/C++/Smalltalk/Forth the examples are written in.

## The two threads that touch this KB

- **Glass, via Tornhill's aside on AI metrics.** He calls out "all the AI adoption metrics being rolled
  out — back to productivity mistaken for lines of code produced, only more elaborate. And expensive."
  A one-line restatement of the measurement critique running through
  [[dora-roi-ai-assisted-software-development-2026]] and his own
  [[tornhill-codescene-unhealthy-code-agentic-token-cost|token-cost work]].
- **Coplien's commonality/variability analysis** — "identify families of systems… capture the domain
  parameters that vary… the foundation of great software design." That is a direct ancestor of the
  [[business-capabilities]] / [[balanced-coupling]] line the KB tracks, and a reminder that
  capability-style boundary-finding predates its current framing by decades.
