# swyx — Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops

AINews post by **swyx** (Latent.Space, 2026-06-12).
Source file: `raw/articles/swyx-loopcraft-art-of-stacking-loops.md` (partial — the post is paywalled;

## The three founding quotes

swyx assembles the "loop discourse" of June 2026:
- **Peter Steinberger (Steipete):** "you shouldn't be prompting coding agents anymore. You should be
  designing loops that prompt your agents."
- **Boris Cherny** (Claude Code, Anthropic): "I don't prompt Claude anymore. I write loops, the loops
  do the work."
- **[[andrej-karpathy|Andrej Karpathy]]** (on Autoresearch): "remove yourself as the bottleneck… You
  have to arrange things such that they're completely autonomous… not be in the loop… increase your
  leverage… I have to arrange it once and hit go." — the same instinct behind the [[llm-wiki]] pattern
  this KB runs on.

## The thesis — stack loops, up and down

"One might argue the entire game of the next century is to be able to **stack loops** as effectively as
possible." The strategic asymmetry: early in each phase it's valuable to know when to go **DOWN** a
loop when things break (for **reliability**); but it's more valuable to know how to go **UP** a loop as
models improve (for **leverage**). This up/down framing is the conceptual core that
[[langchain-the-art-of-loop-engineering|LangChain]] later operationalizes as four concrete stacked
loops.

## The Salty Lesson for agents

A deliberate riff on Richard Sutton's *Bitter Lesson* (which is about models): **"the Salty Lesson for
agents — Don't fix things yourself, as you have done historically. Instead focus on systems that scale
with more agents, like goals and orchestration."** "If you don't figure out how to do this, don't be
salty when you lose to those that do."

## Why it matters here

The naming source for [[loop-engineering]] and the cleanest statement of *why* the layer matters
(leverage via going *up* a loop). Light on mechanics — Osmani supplies the primitives, LangChain the
taxonomy — but it's the primary that anchors the term and gathers the Steinberger/Cherny/Karpathy
provenance in one place. Caveat: paywalled aggregator post; the substantive content is the short free
intro, and the founding quotes are themselves sourced from tweets/talks.
