# Dilger — Trust needs to be engineered

LinkedIn post by **[[martin-dilger]]**, 2026-08-15. Raw capture:
`raw/notes/dilger-trust-needs-to-be-engineered.md`.

**The clearest statement of his answer to the review bottleneck**, and the framing that connects the
whole method to [[unattended-coding-agents]]: don't scale review, make review unnecessary.

## The argument

He opens with a non-developer driving Event Modeling boards via MCP and shipping working slices —
*"they're modeling their complete requirements with AI, and generating working code straight from it."*
Then he states the obvious objection himself, and concedes it:

> "And every engineer thinking now: that's just vibe coding, good luck in production. And to be fair,
> they're not wrong to think it. Code generated by an agent left to its own devices is often crap."

The distinction he draws is **blueprint vs. free structuring**:

> "We don't let the agent freely structuring the system. It's working on a well-defined blueprint
> architecture. The structure is fully defined upfront, through the event model, slicing, before a single
> line of code gets written. All important decisions have been made. The Agent is filling in the blanks,
> doing the boring typing."

And the failure mode if you don't: *"One tiny dependency in the wrong place, a little too much coupling
in another, and that's the start of a downward spiral in quality that is hard to stop."*

## The load-bearing move

> "AI can write good code. The thing is, you just can't trust it to do so reliably. The real issue is
> **trust**."
>
> "Modeling upfront, making the important decisions, defining the structure before anything gets built,
> that's not bureaucracy. It's **engineering trust**… That's how you work around the review bottleneck.
> Not by doing more reviews, but by **making most of them obsolete**."

This is a direct answer to the KB's standing verification problem — [[unattended-coding-agents]],
[[loop-engineering]]'s maker≠checker, and [[software-factory]]'s back-pressure all assume verification
capacity is the binding constraint. Dilger's position is that the constraint is real but attacks the
wrong end: **constrain what the agent can decide, and there is less to verify.** Compare
[[addyosmani-software-factories-light-and-dark]] and the "lights-off" post the next day
([[dilger-lights-off-software-factory-dead-end]]), where he sets out the residual reviews he *does* keep.

## Caveats

- **The proof offered is an anecdote**: "That's the actual proof this works. Not a benchmark, not a
  demo. A non-developer shipping something real." He says so plainly, which is more honest than most of his output, but it remains n=1 and
  unverified.
- Vendor marketing for [[eventmodelers-ai]].
- "Trust engineered into the system" is doing a lot of work as a phrase. It means, concretely, that
  structural decisions are made upfront — not that correctness is guaranteed.
- Also worth noticing what he dislikes and states as motivation: *"who wants to review AI generated code
  anyways? I personally hate it."* The argument is partly an argument from distaste.
