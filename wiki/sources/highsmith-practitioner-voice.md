# Source: Highsmith — Practitioner Voice

Article by **Jim Highsmith** (Agile Manifesto co-author, six books, now in "soft-retirement"), published
on **martinfowler.com**, 2026-08-19. Raw capture: `raw/articles/highsmith-practitioner-voice.md`. Not an
AI or agents piece — it is about **writing**, and it is on-thread because it names the epistemic character
of the source type this KB is mostly built from.

## Summary

Highsmith proposes a name for a writing category he says has existed for a decade without one:
**Practitioner Voice** — distinct from academic writing and from "thought leadership," and now distinct
from a third thing he calls **LLM Voice**. His diagnosis of the first two: *"At their worst, both do the
same thing: they edit consequential human judgment out of the prose. The practitioner disappears behind
the rigor or the polish, and what remains is **correct but weightless**. Academic writing hides behind
rigor. Thought leadership hides behind polish."*

## Key points

**Four defining properties** — read them as a description of what a practitioner primary *is*, and
therefore of what it can and cannot support:

- **Authority from experience, not credentials.** *"Practitioner authority comes from having been in the
  situation, not as a researcher studying it, but as a person responsible for the outcome."* With an
  honest carve-out for consultants: *"We rarely carry direct accountability for outcomes. But if we do our
  work well, we've been close enough to consequence, across enough contexts and enough years, that the
  pattern recognition is real. Earned differently. Still earned."*
- **Tension without resolution.** *"Academic writing resolves tension — that's what conclusions are for.
  Thought leadership resolves it in the three takeaways. Practitioner Voice names the tension and leaves
  it live… Resolving it for them would be lying."*
- **The author stays in.** *"The author's judgment is the point. Not the data. Not the framework."*
- **The readership contract** — the property that matters most for how this KB reads its sources:
  *"It's comfortable with limited context — **this worked here, under these conditions, judge for yourself
  whether it fits yours**. It doesn't chase universality. It trusts specificity."*

**Structure inverted from academic writing:** *"Academic writing builds to the claim. Thought leadership
makes the claim and explains it. Practitioner Voice starts with the claim and trusts the reader to close
the gap."*

**LLM Voice, and the line he says holds.** *"Now AI produces competent writing at scale — fluent,
well-organized, correctly structured, **optimized to say nothing wrong**… The most insidious failure mode
of the three because it's indistinguishable from the real thing until you notice **nothing is at
stake**."* And the distinction he claims survives:

> "AI can mimic the sound of Practitioner Voice. It cannot own the consequences behind it. That's the
> distinction that holds: not style, not compression, not even the rhythm of earned judgment.
> **Accountability.** The willingness to put your name on a call and live with what happens next. That
> can't be automated."

## Limits

- **Assertion, not study.** No evidence of any kind; by his own criteria it is not supposed to have any.
  It is a naming argument, and it should be cited as a *frame*, never as a finding.
- **NOT INDEPENDENT, in two ways worth stating.** It is published on **martinfowler.com**, and
  [[martin-fowler]] is both a character in it (one of the three people who, at three points in his
  career, told Highsmith to *"let your voice out"* — the others were at Cutter Consortium and
  Thoughtworks) and the exemplar of the category (*"Martin Fowler writes this way"*). A piece proposing a
  writing category, hosted by and celebrating the site's owner, is not an arm's-length source on whether
  that category is real or valuable.
- **Its central claim about LLM Voice is contradicted, in this same batch, by
  [[fowler-fragments-2026-09-01]]:** Fowler relays that human ability to distinguish LLM text from human
  text is *"no better than random chance"* in one 2025 study (57%/64% recognition in another). Highsmith's
  argument depends on the reader noticing that "nothing is at stake." If detection is near chance, then
  **accountability is a property of the author, not a signal in the text** — which weakens the practical
  version of his claim while leaving the normative version intact. Hold both.
- **Self-undercutting detail, and he discloses it:** *"Parts of this essay were edited with assistance
  from an AI language model (Claude Sonnet 4.6). I reviewed and approved the final text and am solely
  responsible for its content."* That is precisely the accountability move he says cannot be automated —
  which is either the argument's best demonstration or its neatest irony, depending on how charitable you
  are. Both readings are available; the KB should not pick one.
- The promised follow-up ("the craft — a guide to writing in Practitioner Voice") is unwritten.

## Connections / contrast

- **Directly useful to this KB's own house rules.** The `CLAUDE.md` interested-claim markers include
  *"impression, not measurement"* with the note that "the practitioner closest to a thing usually notices
  it first." Highsmith supplies the genre-level account of *why* that is true and of what such a source
  offers: pattern recognition earned by consequence, presented as *"this worked here, under these
  conditions."* His readership contract is close to a specification for how to read the KB's Dilger,
  Osmani, Miller, Fritzsche, Hashimoto and Willison primaries — and for why the correct caveat is usually
  "limited context, judge fit," not "unproven, discount."
- **It also names the KB's own risk.** A compiler that summarizes practitioner primaries into a wiki
  *removes the author from the writing* — the exact failure Highsmith diagnoses. A page that flattens a
  named practitioner's live tension into a resolved bullet has laundered more than confidence; it has
  laundered the genre. The KB's practice of quoting practitioners verbatim and of keeping disagreements
  open ("when sources conflict, say so explicitly") is the counter-measure, and this source is the
  argument for keeping it.
- **The "tension without resolution" property is a source-selection heuristic**, and it cuts against
  material in this batch: [[swyx-gpt6-astra-automated-ai-engineer]] resolves every tension it raises;
  [[miller-pondering-continuous-integration-ai-world-order]] leaves its central one live.
- Compare [[laycock-citizens-build-agents-execute-experts-govern]] and
  [[fritzsche-what-ai-changes-is-which-work-stays-hard]]: three independent 2026 statements that the
  residual scarce thing is accountable judgement. Highsmith's version is about who can *write*
  credibly; the others are about who can *decide*. Same claim, different surface — and none of the three
  measures anything.
- Kevlin Henney's skippable-LinkedIn-post heuristic in [[fowler-fragments-2026-08-24]] ("no voice of
  poster… aspiring anodyne anonymity") is the same diagnosis in one line.
