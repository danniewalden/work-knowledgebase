# Dilger — The harness is 20%; the first 80% is understanding the problem

LinkedIn post by **[[martin-dilger]]** (2026-06-29; captured same day via logged-in Chrome).
Source file: `raw/notes/dilger-harness-is-20-percent-requirements-are-80.md`. Tagged
`#eventmodeling`; two outbound links (a video and a free-apply link) not captured.

## What it says

A pointed critique of the current **[[harness-engineering|harness]]** discourse from the
[[event-modeling]] side. Dilger's argument:

- Building harnesses for AI-assisted development is "the same mistake we engineers always do — throwing
  technology at problems that can't be solved with technology alone." The people doing it are mostly
  excellent; that's not the issue.
- **The harness and the code are only ~20% of the solution.** The other **80% is understanding what
  problem you're solving** — clarifying requirements, understanding business processes, and getting them
  into a format that works.
- No amount of agents, roles, skills, and guardrails rescues unclear, blurry, gap-filled requirements:
  "the result will be mediocre at best." The chainsaw metaphor — a fresh chainsaw is useless if nobody
  told you which trees to cut.
- "I spent weeks crafting this beautiful harness … just specify your requirements as markdown and go" —
  **but nobody tells you how to write that damn markdown**, or how much detail is necessary. That has
  always been the hard part, and **there is no technical solution to it — it's a human, communications
  problem.**
- He focuses his own work on the **first 80%** (i.e. Event Modeling), after which "the rest becomes
  pretty easy if the problem is cleared." His wry diagnosis of *why* the field over-invests in the 20%:
  "building the harness, taming the agent is way more fun."

## Why it matters here

This crystallizes Dilger's running thesis into an explicit **20/80 framing** that connects the two
biggest threads in the KB: it accepts [[harness-engineering]] as real but **subordinate** to the
requirements/spec problem that [[event-modeling]] + [[spec-driven-development]] address. It's the same
"AI amplifies unclear requirements" point ([[dilger-faros-ai-report-amplifies-unclear-requirements]],
[[dilger-is-code-still-the-source-of-truth]]) recast as a quantified split, and an unusually direct
shot across the bow of the harness-engineering community ([[birgitta-bockeler]], [[mitchell-hashimoto]],
[[stripe-minions-one-shot-coding-agents]]) — not "harnesses are wrong" but "harnesses are the easy 20%."

## Caveats

LinkedIn post, marketing-adjacent (links to a video and his free-to-apply tool). The 20/80 split is
rhetorical, not measured. The harness sources would partly agree — Böckeler's own framing is that a good
harness can't *force* a non-deterministic model either — so the disagreement is one of emphasis (where
the leverage is) more than of fact.
