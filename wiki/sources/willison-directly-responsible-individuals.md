# Willison — Directly Responsible Individuals (DRI)

Link-blog post by **[[simon-willison]]** (simonwillison.net, 2026-07-12).
Source file: `raw/articles/willison-directly-responsible-individuals.md`. Filed by the
2026-07-13 headless watch (in-window, on the agent-accountability seam).

## Why it matters

A short post but a clean, quotable thesis on **who is accountable when an agent does the work** —
the human-ownership question that [[loop-engineering]]'s "stay the engineer" caveat and
[[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop|Osmani's "Own the Outer Loop"]] (Answerability) circle around, stated
here at its sharpest.

## Core thesis

- Willison anchors the term **Directly Responsible Individual (DRI)** — the person "ultimately
  accountable for the success or failure of a specific project, initiative, or activity" (origin: Apple;
  best public definition he found: the GitLab handbook).
- **An LLM agent should *never* be a project's DRI.** Accountability "feels uniquely human… because
  humans can take accountability for their actions where machines cannot."
- He ties it to **IBM's 1979 training slide**: *"A computer can never be held accountable, therefore a
  computer must never make a management decision."* The point survives 47 years of hardware.

## Relevance / links

Complements [[addyosmani-own-the-outer-loop]] directly: Osmani's third demand, **Answerability**
("guarantee you can explain *why*") and his insistence that ownership stays with the human, is the same
claim from the loop-engineering side; Willison supplies the crisp accountability formulation and the
historical anchor. Extends [[loop-engineering]]'s standing caveat ("build it like someone who intends to
stay the engineer, not just the person who presses go") and touches [[agent-explainability]]
(answerability ↔ the recorded *why*). A boundary condition on the [[autonomy-ladder]]: however far
autonomy climbs, the DRI slot does not transfer to the machine.
