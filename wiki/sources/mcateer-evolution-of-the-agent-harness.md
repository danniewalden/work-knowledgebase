# Source: The Evolution of the Agent Harness (Dan McAteer)

**[[dan-mcateer|Dan McAteer]], guest post on *Latent.Space*, 2026-08-22 (retrieved 2026-08-26). Raw capture: `raw/articles/mcateer-evolution-of-the-agent-harness.md`** (source_url latent.space/p/attention-interface). Engagement at capture: 135 likes, 13 restacks.

**This is the one item in this batch that is NOT an arXiv preprint** — it is a practitioner essay, and it is the batch's only *authored narrative* of how the harness got here. Its argument is strong and its numbers are all **secondhand and unlinked in the capture**; the two need separating carefully, and this page does so.

## Summary

McAteer's answer to "what changed around Christmas 2025, when agents suddenly started to work?" is that **neither the model nor the harness jumped — their two improvement curves crossed.** He quotes Transformer co-inventor **Lukasz Kaiser** (on *Unsupervised Learning*, "in June" — the capture gives no year; the essay is 2026-08-22): "the harness changed and a little post-training changed and then new pre-trained models came… but it felt like a big jump which is not that easy to pin down what did it." McAteer's claim: "The answer to 'What happened?' isn't solely in the model weights. It's in the system that grew up around the weights."

He then frames the whole history as **two curves — "what the harness asks of the model, and what the model can deliver in practice" — whose gap *is* agent effectiveness**, and reads four eras off it (Harness 1.0), before naming the present dynamic (2.0) and predicting its endpoint (3.0).

**The load-bearing idea is the absorption loop: train → absorb → shed → repeat.** Models are now trained *inside* their harness, so they absorb harness capabilities into their weights; the harness then sheds the scaffold that got absorbed; the model climbs to the next thing it cannot do. His metric follows directly: "**The measure of the pace of agent harness evolution is how much of the harness you get to delete, while retaining the same capability level.**"

And the conclusion that gives the piece its URL: absorption does not end the harness, it **inverts** it. "The harness was born as the human interface to the model… The harness becomes the model's interface to our human attention." He calls that the **attention-interface**.

## Key points

- **Harness 1.0, "The Bolt-On Era" — four stages, each a gap-closing move:**
  - **ReAct (Oct 2022), "the harness on paper"** — the reason → act → observe loop as a *prompting technique*, external to the weights; nobody calls it a harness yet. Toolformer (Meta, Feb 2023) "hints that tool use could be trained in rather than prompted."
  - **AutoGPT/BabyAGI (spring 2023), "premature autonomy"** — the harness curve sprints ahead of the model. His mechanism is the argument, not the anecdote: "**A loop doesn't add capability to a model. A loop amplifies the capability a model has, and below some threshold the loop amplifies errors rather than reliability.**" With the arithmetic: 95% per-step reliability over 20 steps ≈ 36% success. This is the cleanest available statement of why [[ralph-loop|loops]] fail below a capability threshold and belongs on the KB's loop pages.
  - **Cursor/Copilot (2023–24), "retreat to human in the loop"** — close the gap by pulling the *harness* below the model; give the human the loop. Devin's first version tried to hand autonomy back and an Answer.AI test put it at **~15% success** *(secondhand, no link in the capture)*, which McAteer reads as evidence the IDEs' retreat "is not cowardly, but the correct move." Then o1 (late 2024) inverts the gap and creates a **model capability overhang**.
  - **Claude Code (Feb 2025), "the curves cross"** — terminal instead of IDE, bash and file access, **permission rules instead of per-change approval**; "the model is handed the loop again, and this time it understands the assignment." Built "with the next model's capabilities in mind, not the current one." He attributes its success to *timing*, not novelty — and cites roughly **$1B ARR within six months** *([[anthropic]] product figure, secondhand and unlinked in the capture — a vendor-scale claim, not a measurement)*.
- **Harness 2.0, "The Co-Training Era" — the present.** RL has moved *inside* the harness (quoting OpenAI's codex-1 announcement, May 2025: "codex-1 was trained using reinforcement learning on real-world coding tasks in a variety of environments"), so the two curves "braid". Then absorption: the **GPT-5.1-Codex-Max** launch line, "The first model natively trained to operate across multiple context windows through compaction" — auto-compaction moving from harness code into weights. And the shedding: **Thariq Shihipar ([[anthropic]]) said the team "recently deleted 80% of Claude Code's system prompt"** *(secondhand vendor statement, no link in the capture; a claim about a private artifact that cannot be verified from outside — IMPRESSION, NOT MEASUREMENT)*. **This one datum does have in-KB corroboration:** [[willison-fireside-chat-claude-code-team]] records the Claude Code team (Cat Wu / Thariq) saying the system prompt shrank ~80% for frontier models, with a different system prompt per model — still [[anthropic]]'s own statement about its own product (**vendor self-report**), but first-hand rather than relayed, and it adds the detail McAteer's version omits: the shrink is *per frontier model*, not a global simplification.
- **The two quantitative anchors, both secondhand — flagged inline wherever the KB uses them:**
  - **Harness-Bench:** "ran the same model over the same **106 tasks** in different harnesses, and scores ranged from **52.4 to 76.2**: a **23.8-point spread with zero change to the model**." His gloss: "**Half the agent is the harness.**" *(Cited without a link or author in the capture; the KB holds no capture of Harness-Bench itself. Treat as an unverified secondhand benchmark figure — it is the batch's most-quoted number and the least-sourced.)*
  - **ARC-AGI-3:** "**GPT-5.6 Sol's ARC-AGI-3 score tripled from 13.3% to 38.3%**" by "adding only retained reasoning and compaction". *([[openai]]'s own result about its own model — **VENDOR SELF-REPORT**; also secondhand and unlinked in the capture.)*
- **Harness 3.0, "The Attention Era" — the prediction.** Keep deleting whatever the model absorbs (he expects multi-agent orchestration, tool selection and memory to go next; notes that "researchers are building self-improving harnesses that can themselves be trained in a similar way to models" — which is precisely this batch's harness-evolution cluster). What survives is what no model can absorb: **permissions, identity, trust, legibility**. "A model that absorbs permissions into itself has dissolved permissions."
- **The scarcity argument, quoting Ryan Lopopolo** (Latent Space, "Extreme Harness Engineering for Token Billionaires"): "**The only fundamentally scarce thing is the synchronous human attention of my team.**" Tokens became abundant; attention did not. So the gap does not vanish — it **migrates across the human boundary**, becoming "the space between what the agent asks of the human, and what the human is able to answer."
- **The falsifiable prediction, with a date:** "within a year, every company building agentic AI will ship a **human attention policy surface** in the way that every agentic AI company shipped AGENTS.md." It governs "when it's allowed to interrupt you, when it should keep working, which decisions it can make alone and which decisions need your approval" — and becomes "a learnable component of the system… Every correction becomes useful data." Current sparks he names: Anthropic's long-running-agent progress files and agentic approval queues. **Worth diarising: prediction made 2026-08-22, testable ~2027-08.**

## Limits

- **Not a paper and not peer-reviewed** — a guest essay on a newsletter, i.e. argued opinion. Its value is the *frame* (two curves; train→absorb→shed; the inversion), not evidence.
- **Every number in it is secondhand and, in this capture, unlinked.** Harness-Bench (52.4→76.2 over 106 tasks) has no author, venue or link in the capture and **no primary capture exists in this KB**; the ARC-AGI-3 tripling is an [[openai]] self-report; the 80%-system-prompt deletion is a secondhand statement about a closed artifact; Devin's ~15% and Claude Code's $1B ARR are likewise uncorroborated here. **All of these must carry their marker at every use on every page.** Finding and capturing Harness-Bench is the single highest-value follow-up this page implies.
- **The two-curves model is a narrative device, not a measured quantity.** Neither curve is defined operationally or plotted from data; the "crossing" is an interpretation of a chronology. It explains well and predicts loosely.
- **Selection bias toward coding agents and toward Anthropic/OpenAI products.** The eras are told through Claude Code, Codex and Cursor; nothing in it tests whether the same absorption dynamic holds for non-coding agents.
- **The absorption thesis has an obvious unexamined counter-argument, which the KB should hold beside it:** [[langchain]]'s Harrison Chase argues the opposite direction — better models *expand* what harnesses must do rather than shrinking them (Claude Code at 512k+ LOC and growing, per [[langchain-anatomy-of-an-agent-harness]]). McAteer's own "delete 80% of the system prompt" datum is compatible with both (prompt shrinks, codebase grows). **The KB holds two contradictory trend claims about harness size and should say so rather than choose.**
- Engagement metrics (135 likes) are a popularity datum, not a quality one.
