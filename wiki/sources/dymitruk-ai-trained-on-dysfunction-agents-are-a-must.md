# Adam Dymitruk — AI was trained on dysfunction, so agents are a must

Short LinkedIn post by **[[adam-dymitruk]]** (2026-06-15; captured same day via logged-in Chrome).
Source file: `raw/notes/dymitruk-ai-trained-on-dysfunction-agents-are-a-must.md`.

## What it says

The full argument, near-verbatim: AI won't help you write better software *on its own* because the
industry's idea of "best practices and architectures" is dysfunctional — and **AI was trained on that
dysfunction**. However, you *can* piece together systems that follow best practices, so **agents are a
must** for assembling them. He points to **[[martin-dilger]]** and **[[yordis-prieto]]** as "the best
way to achieve this."

## Why it matters here

A one-line statement of the *why* behind [[event-modeled-agent-design]] from the method's creator: the
value isn't the model's training prior (it's polluted) but the **scaffolding you impose** — the event
model / spec that constrains the agent toward best-practice structure. This is the same
"design-the-environment-not-the-prompt" thesis [[martin-dilger]] argues
([[dilger-spec-driven-development-applied]]), now explicitly endorsed by Dymitruk and tied to a named
peer group. Notable as a **watch-list signal**: it surfaces **[[yordis-prieto]]** as a new voice worth
tracking on the EM × agents focus.

## Caveats

A throwaway-length opinion post, not an argued piece; "trained on dysfunction" is an assertion, not
evidence. Its value is as a pointer (to people and to a stance), not as a source of detail.
