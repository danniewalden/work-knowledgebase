# Dilger — Event Modeling is a shift left on the organization

LinkedIn post by **[[martin-dilger]]**, 2026-08-25. Raw capture:
`raw/notes/dilger-event-modeling-is-a-shift-left-on-the-organization.md`. 12 reactions, one from
[[adam-dymitruk]].

**The adoption-side complement to every method post in the KB** — and a rare admission that the method
being simple is not the point.

## The argument

> "'But Martin, you always tell us Event Modeling is so simple!' And it is. I can explain Event Modeling
> in five minutes… For a long time I thought that was the hard part. Get the notation right, get the
> sticky notes moving, and the rest follows. **It doesn't.** The 'problem' was never the methodology.
> It's everything that comes with it."

The reframing:

> "Event Modeling isn't a training problem. It's a **gigantic shift left on the organization**. You solve
> the hard problems while they're still cheap, not months later when the assumptions are buried in code."

And the mechanism cost: *"Making Modeling real means roles change, the organizational setup shifts, the
way of working is never the same again. This is one of the biggest levers a company has for AI
Integration. None of it comes for free."*

## What he says you actually need

Four items, and he is pointed about the first being insufficient on its own:

- **Clear methodology to drive focused workshops** — "1x/2x 30min a week is enough."
- **Tooling** — "don't force teams onto whiteboards, we spent months building tooling that saves hours a
  day per engineer."
- **Training & facilitation.**
- **Organizational buy-in** — "start bottom-up, but you'll need it eventually."

His jab at the alternative is the most quotable line: an article listing "Collaborative Design Sessions"
as one bullet among many, *"as if inviting everyone to a meeting makes them work together. Those people
have never worked in a company it seems."*

## Why it matters here

- **It is the honest counterweight to the KB's tooling-heavy Event Modeling thread.** Nearly every other
  Dilger capture is about artifacts, formats and agents; this one says the artifact is the easy part and
  the organisation is the hard part. That bears directly on [[conways-law]] and on
  [[event-modeled-agent-design]]'s standing weak point — the claim that models are built collaboratively
  with stakeholders in the room is a claim about *practice*, and this post is the closest the KB has to
  an account of what that practice costs.
- It supplies a concrete cadence (30 minutes, once or twice a week) where the KB otherwise has none.
- **"Solve the hard problems while they're still cheap"** is the flat-cost-curve argument
  ([[triplet-architecture]]) restated as an organisational rather than architectural property.
- Note the tension with [[ng-spec-driven-development-is-waterfall-in-markdown]]'s provenance objection:
  Ng's charge is that one person's spec gets passed off as shared understanding. Dilger's answer here is
  organisational change rather than a better artifact — which concedes that the artifact alone does not
  answer it.

## Caveats

- Consulting marketing, ending in two calls to action; "we have the numbers to help" is asserted and no
  numbers appear.
- "Dozens of teams… most tell me the same thing: they never want to go back" is unattributed and
  self-selected.
- "The red pill" framing is doing rhetorical work.
