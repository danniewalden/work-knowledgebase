# Wardley Maps — Value Chains & The Four Stages of Evolution

The **Wardley primary** the KB wanted: two foundational chapters of Simon Wardley's *Wardley Maps:
Topographical intelligence in business* (Ch.2 Value Chains, Ch.3 Evolution), as hosted and **adapted &
expanded by Krzysztof "Chris" Daniel** on WardleyMaps.com (CC BY-SA 4.0). Source file:
`raw/articles/wardley-maps-value-chains-and-evolution.md`. Useful bonus: WardleyMaps.com is maintained
by **[[chris-daniel]]** — the same person behind the [[daniel-event-modeling-wardley-mapping|EM × Wardley series]] —
so this doubles as Daniel's own expanded treatment of the method.

## The two foundations

- **Value chain (Ch.2).** Every business has a value chain — the sequence of activities that create
  value for customers. **Start from the user need** ("we help teams collaborate," not "we sell
  software") and **work backwards** through the components required to deliver it (user → channel →
  value proposition → key activities/resources/partners → cost). Components depend on and influence each
  other; a change in one ripples through the chain. The vertical axis of a map is **visibility to the
  user** along this chain.
- **Evolution (Ch.3).** Every component evolves left-to-right through four stages — **Genesis →
  Custom Built → Product → Commodity** — from novel/uncertain/changing to ubiquitous/standardized/
  utility-like. This is the horizontal axis of a map.

## The strategic payoff — treat components by where they are *and* where they're going

The map's power is the **evolution → action** rule: **invest/differentiate** on Genesis & Custom-Built
components (competitive advantage lives there); **optimize or outsource/buy** Product & Commodity ones
(standardized, cost-effective). Monitor components moving rightward to anticipate openings and threats.
"The same component can be strategic in one context and commoditized in another" — context is everything.

## Why it matters here

This grounds the [[wardley-mapping]] stub on the actual method instead of a second-hand series summary,
and sharpens the **capabilities × strategy** trio Dannie flagged: [[business-capabilities]] name the
stable *what* (a capability is a value-chain component), Wardley evolution tells you *how to treat each
one strategically* (build the differentiating ones, buy the commodity ones), and [[event-modeling]]
renders the chosen direction as a concrete design — the [[daniel-event-modeling-wardley-mapping|Daniel
thesis]] "Wardley = where to go and why; Event Modeling = what to build." The build-vs-buy-by-evolution
lens is also directly relevant to **agent ownership**: differentiating capabilities are where bespoke
agent work pays off; commodity ones should lean on utilities.

## Caveats

The hosted text is Daniel's *adaptation and expansion* of Wardley's prose (CC BY-SA), not a
byte-for-byte reproduction of the original Medium book — faithful to the method but lightly editorialized.
Two chapters only; doctrine, climatic patterns, gameplay, and the pioneers/settlers/town-planners (PST)
model are not captured here.
